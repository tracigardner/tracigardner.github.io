Keep It Together

Page Breaks & the Principle of Proximity

A page break should not break the reader’s understanding. When a heading, paragraph, table, or caption gets separated from related information, readers must work harder to follow the document.

The Big Idea: Proximity Shows Relationships

Information that belongs together should appear together. Use page breaks and white space to make those relationships clear, even when that means leaving some room at the bottom of a page.

Other Relationships Worth Protecting

Lists & Introductions

Keep a sentence that introduces a list with at least the beginning of that list. Avoid isolating short list items.

Warnings & Instructions

Place cautions, notes, and explanations next to the steps or information they concern.

Visuals & Discussion

Place figures and tables near the text that introduces or explains them, whenever practical.

Not Every Page Break Is a Problem

Long paragraphs and tables sometimes need to continue onto another page. Make the continuation readable. A little extra white space is better than separating information that belongs together.

The Page-Break Check

  • Do headings stay with their content?
  • Are single paragraph lines stranded?
  • Are tables readable across pages?
  • Do captions stay with their visuals?
  • Do lists, notes, and explanations stay near related content?
  • Have you checked the finished pages, not just the draft view?
