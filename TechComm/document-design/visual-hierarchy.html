Document Design Quick Reference

What Gets Read First?

Visual hierarchy tells readers what matters most, what comes next, and where to look for details. Good design creates a clear reading path before readers begin reading closely.

1

Size

Larger elements usually attract attention before smaller ones. Reserve the largest type for the most important information.

detail → heading → TITLE
2

Weight

Bold type creates emphasis. Use it selectively so important words stand out instead of making everything compete.

regular  →  important

5

Additional Details

Supporting information belongs lower in the hierarchy. Readers who need more detail can continue reading without having to search through it for the main message.

3

Placement

Readers often notice information near the top of a page and at the beginning of sections first. Put important information where readers can find it quickly.

4

Contrast

Differences in size, color, shape, or style help important information stand apart from surrounding content.

ordinary → NOTICE ME
5

White Space

Space separates groups and gives important content room to stand out. Crowded pages make everything look equally important.

Design the Reading Order

Decide what readers should notice first, second, and third. Then use visual hierarchy to guide them there. If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.
