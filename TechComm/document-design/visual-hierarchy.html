What Gets Read First?

Visual hierarchy helps readers see what matters most, what comes next, and where to find supporting details. The left column shows an example of what it looks like. The right column explain the strategies the example uses.

Additional Details

Supporting information can appear lower in the hierarchy. Readers who need more detail can continue reading without searching through it for the main message.

5 Ways to Create Hierarchy

Size

Larger elements attract attention first. Save the largest type for what matters most.

TITLE → heading → detail

Weight

Bold text creates emphasis. Use it selectively so key words stand out.

important  →  regular

Placement

Put important information near the top of the page or at the start of a section.

KEY MESSAGE
additional information

Contrast

Differences in color, size, shape, or style make important information stand out.

ordinary → NOTICE ME

White Space

Space separates groups and gives important information room to stand out.

headingtextdetailheadingtextdetail Key Message

Design the Reading Order

Use size, weight, placement, contrast, and white space to show readers what matters most. If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.
