Headings

A Roadmap for Your Reader

Readers rarely travel through a document one sentence at a time. They scan for signs that tell them where they are, what lies ahead, and how the information fits together. Strong headings provide that map.

Give Readers a Route Through the Document

Think of each heading as a signpost. A useful sign names the destination, helps readers choose a route, and shows how one part of the document connects to the next. A reader who scans only the headings should still be able to tell where the document is going.

Stop 1: Destination Name what readers will find.
Stop 2: Route Help readers scan and navigate.
Stop 3: Hierarchy Show how sections fit together.
1

Name the Destination

Write content-rich headings that tell readers where each section leads.

Vague Signposts
Background A sign marked only “Background” does not tell readers where they are headed.
Results Readers have to enter the section before they can tell which results are there.
Discussion The label names a section type, but not its destination.
Useful Destinations
Current Water Usage in Montgomery County Readers know exactly what information this section contains.
Survey Results: Resident Water Concerns The heading names both the evidence and its focus.
Implications for Future Water Management The heading previews the direction the discussion will take.
2

Mark the Route

Place useful signposts along the way so readers can scan, orient themselves, and jump to what they need.

Few Route Markers

Energy Use in Data Centers

Data centers require significant amounts of electricity to operate. This section provides information about how data centers use energy, the factors that influence their consumption, and the potential impacts on the local power grid. It also discusses strategies for improving efficiency and reducing overall energy demand.

Ways to reduce energy use and support sustainability.

3

Show the Route Hierarchy

Use heading levels consistently so readers can distinguish major destinations from smaller stops along the route.

1. Project Overview
1.1 Purpose
1.2 Scope
2. Key Findings
2.1 Current Conditions
Local Trends

Major Destinations: Level 1

  • Most visually prominent heading level.
  • Mark the document’s major sections.
  • Use one consistent style for every major destination.

Stops Along the Route: Level 2

  • Visually subordinate to Level 1.
  • Mark important stops within a major section.
  • Stay distinct from both Level 1 and body text.

Local Turns: Level 3

  • Less prominent than Level 2.
  • Show smaller turns within a subsection.
  • Use only when the route truly needs another level.

Try the Roadmap Test

Read only the headings, from top to bottom. Can you tell where the document starts, what its major destinations are, how the sections connect, and where to go for a specific piece of information? If not, revise the map.
