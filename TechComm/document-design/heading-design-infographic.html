Effective Headings

Help Readers Find, Skim, and Understand

Headings do more than decorate a page. They tell readers what information is available, show how ideas fit together, and create a roadmap through the document.

Think of Headings as Navigation

A reader should be able to scan the headings and quickly understand the document’s structure, major topics, and path through the information.

1

Write Clear, Content-Rich Headings

Tell readers what they will find, not just what kind of section they are looking at.

Too Vague
Background Doesn’t tell readers what background information is covered.
Results Readers have to enter the section to discover which results matter.
Discussion Gives readers almost no clue about the section’s content.
Clear + Content-Rich
Current Water Usage in Montgomery County Names the specific background topic.
Survey Results: Resident Water Concerns Identifies exactly which results are discussed.
Implications for Future Water Management Signals the focus of the discussion.
2

Use Headings to Help Readers Skim

Break large ideas into meaningful sections so readers can see the document’s main points at a glance.

Hard to Skim

Energy Use in Data Centers

Data centers require significant amounts of electricity to operate. This section provides information about how data centers use energy, the factors that influence their consumption, and the potential impacts on the local power grid. It also discusses strategies for improving efficiency and reducing overall energy demand.

Easy to Skim

Energy Use in Data Centers

How Data Centers Use Energy

Overview of electricity needs and key systems.

Factors That Influence Energy Consumption

Size, operations, and cooling requirements.

Potential Impacts on the Local Power Grid

What increased demand could mean for the region.

Strategies for Improving Efficiency

Ways to reduce energy use and support sustainability.

3

Style Headings to Show Document Hierarchy

Use consistent formatting so readers can see which sections are major, which are subordinate, and how ideas relate.

1. Project Overview
1.1 Purpose
1.2 Scope
2. Key Findings
2.1 Current Conditions
Local Trends

Main Headings: Level 1

  • Largest and most prominent.
  • Identify major document sections.
  • Use the same style throughout.

Subheadings: Level 2

  • Smaller than Level 1 headings.
  • Divide a major section into related parts.
  • Remain clearly distinct from body text.

Lower-Level Headings: Level 3

  • Less prominent than Level 2.
  • Signal another level of detail when needed.
  • Follow a consistent visual pattern.

Test Your Headings

Read only the headings. Can you understand the document’s structure and main ideas without reading the body text?
