Integrating Visual Evidence

Make figures and tables part of your explanation—not just something readers look at.

A figure or table can present important evidence, but it cannot make your point on its own. Help readers understand what they are seeing, why it matters, and how it supports your message.

How It Works

Review of the Employee Onboarding Guide

The guide uses similar formatting for headings at different levels. Figure 1 shows an example from the guide.

1. Introduce

Mention the figure before it appears. Tell readers what they are about to see and why it is relevant.

2. Label

Give the figure a number and informative caption. Place figure captions below figures; identify sources when needed.

The heading levels use nearly identical type sizes and weights, making it difficult to distinguish major topics from supporting information.

3. Explain

Point out the pattern, comparison, or detail that matters. Go beyond repeating the caption.

As Figure 1 illustrates, the guide would benefit from more distinct heading styles so readers can locate procedures more easily.

4. Connect

Use the evidence to support a conclusion, recommendation, or other point. Refer to the figure by number.

Notice the connection: The introduction prepares readers for the figure; the caption identifies it; the explanation interprets what it shows; and the final sentence uses that evidence to make a point.

3. Explain

Interpret the pattern or comparison that matters rather than simply repeating the table entries.

These findings support adopting a consistent formatting system to help readers recognize different kinds of information.

4. Connect

Use the findings to support a conclusion or recommendation that advances your message.

Figures and tables follow the same principles. Number them in separate sequences (Figure 1, Figure 2; Table 1, Table 2). A common convention places figure captions below figures and table titles above tables. Follow the style required for your document. Include appropriate source credit and accessible descriptions.
