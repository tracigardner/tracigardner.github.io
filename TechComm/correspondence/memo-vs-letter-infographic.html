Memos vs. Letters

Choosing the Right Format for Workplace Communication

Both memos and letters are forms of workplace correspondence. They differ in purpose, audience, tone, and format.

Memos
Letters

Workplace Communication

Used to share information, make requests, report findings, document decisions, or communicate recommendations.

Purpose

External Communication

Commonly used to communicate with people or organizations outside the company.

Internal and Client Communication

Frequently used with internal audiences such as employees, managers, departments, and teams. Memos may also be exchanged between a company and clients or other external contacts.

Audience

External Audiences

Written for clients, customers, vendors, partners, government agencies, organizations, or members of the public.

Direct and Concise

Usually straightforward and focused on the information, request, or action the reader needs.

Tone

Professional and Courteous

Often uses a more formal and relationship-focused tone, especially when representing the organization to outside readers.

Memo Format

Uses identifying headers rather than an inside address, salutation, complimentary closing, and signature block.

Format

Business Letter Format

Typically includes an inside address, salutation, body, complimentary closing, and signature.

    • From

  • Body
Key Parts

Key Parts

  • Heading or letterhead
  • Inside address
  • Salutation
  • Body
  • Closing
  • Signature

Usually Short

Often designed for quick reading and focused on the information the reader needs.

Length

Can Be Short or Longer

Length depends on the purpose, reader, and complexity of the message.

Use a Memo When...

You need to inform, request, explain, recommend, or report to someone inside the organization or to a client when a memo is the appropriate established format.

Use When

Use a Letter When...

You need formal external correspondence such as an inquiry, response, notification, request, or relationship-building message.

Choose the Format That Fits Your Audience and Purpose

Clear structure + appropriate tone + reader awareness = effective workplace communication.

Good Communication

  • Builds understanding.
  • Supports action.
  • Strengthens relationships.

Write Clearly

Keep the reader, purpose, and workplace context at the center of your choices.

Good Choice

  • Right message.
  • Right format.
  • Right impact.
