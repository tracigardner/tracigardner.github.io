Make a Mess, but Leave Yourself Clues

A rough draft doesn't have to be pretty. When you encounter a missing detail, leave yourself a specific note and keep writing.

Here's what that might look like in the Results section of a Recommendation Report. Notice how the writer uses bracketed reminders to mark unfinished details without interrupting the draft.

Results: Heading Consistency Working Draft

The guide uses similar formatting for headings at different levels, making the hierarchy difficult to recognize.

[Add screenshot showing heading levels]

This inconsistency may make it harder for readers to locate instructions.

[Find design guideline and add citation]
Keep the writing moving. Your notes will tell you what to finish when you revise.

Is It a Finding or an Opinion?

Your Results section should document problems, not just describe what you dislike. Consider the difference:

Opinion

“The guide’s headings are confusing.”

Finding

“Major and minor headings use nearly identical formatting.”

The second statement identifies a problem you can document with a screenshot and evaluate against design principles.

Try it: Can you point to specific evidence supporting each finding in your Results section?

Don't Make Your Reader Connect the Dots

Your client hasn't followed every step of your investigation. Show how each recommendation grows from an identified problem and its consequences.

1. Problem

Heading levels look almost identical.

2. Consequence

Readers struggle to recognize the document's hierarchy.

3. Recommendation

Use distinct, consistent styles for each heading level.

Check your draft: Can readers trace each recommendation back to a documented problem?
