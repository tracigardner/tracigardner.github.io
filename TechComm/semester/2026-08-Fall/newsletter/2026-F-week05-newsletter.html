Don’t Shrink It—Edit It

Your learning aid has to fit on one page. If it doesn’t, your first instinct may be to make everything smaller.

But tiny text and cramped spacing solve a length problem by creating a readability problem.

Before shrinking anything, cut repetition, shorten long explanations, combine related ideas, and remove information your reader doesn’t really need.

You can also let lists, tables, diagrams, or other useful visuals carry information more efficiently than another paragraph.

If the page is still overloaded, you may simply be trying to cover too many concepts.

When the page is too full, make the content smarter before you make the type smaller. You’ll find more advice in this week’s reading on how to Fit It on One Page.

A Rough Draft Should Actually Be Rough

The goal of a rough draft isn’t perfection. It’s to create enough of the document to see what needs to change next.

Once you have a draft, you can test the document as a complete learning aid to determine what you need to revise.

Revise

Your test may reveal problems you couldn’t see earlier.

Look for issues such as these:

  • crowded or empty sections
  • confusing organization
  • weak or unnecessary visuals
  • missing information
  • content that takes too much space

As you move to the revision stage, these larger problems matter more than polishing a sentence or adjusting a tiny design detail. They let you focus on substantial revision.

Help Your Reader Learn Without You

Try giving your learning aid or visual glossary to someone unfamiliar with the topic. Don’t explain it to them first. Let the document do the work so you can test its effectiveness.

Test Your User Document

After your reader reviews your document, consider these questions:

  • Does your reader understand the concepts?
  • Can they tell why the ideas matter?
  • Can they recognize examples?
  • Can they figure out how to apply what they learned?

If you notice your reader struggling, you’ve found something to revise.

Bottom Line

A successful user document shouldn’t need its author standing beside it.

The page itself should teach readers what they need to know.
