You Don’t Need to Know the Answer Yet

You may already notice problems in your chosen client document. That’s a good place to start—but not a reason to jump straight to a solution.

Your proposal comes before the full investigation. Right now, you have observations and questions. Your later recommendation report will be based on the evidence you gather and analyze.

Notice
Investigate
Recommend

So instead of writing, “The headings are ineffective and should be replaced,” propose to investigate whether the heading structure helps readers locate the information they need.

In the proposal, explain what you need to find out—not what you have already decided.

The evidence may confirm your first impression, complicate it, or point you somewhere else. That’s what the investigation is for.

Your Real Topic Is the Document

The scenarios cover interesting subjects—food safety, cybersecurity, housing development, data centers, engineering, and more. But your job is not to solve the problem discussed in the document.

Keep the Focus on Communication

  • Don’t decide whether the subdivision should be built.
  • Don’t develop a new cybersecurity policy.
  • Don’t decide whether the county should support data centers.
  • Do investigate how effectively the document communicates with its intended users.

For example, the data-center scenario specifically asks you to evaluate how effectively the policy brief communicates, not whether Montgomery County should encourage or restrict data-center development.

The subject gives you the context. The communication document is your project.
