Revision Is More Than Editing

When you’re getting a document ready to submit, it’s easy to start fixing sentences, punctuation, and tiny formatting details.

Those things matter—but editing isn’t the same as revising.

Revise
Edit
Proofread

Revise first: Look at the big picture. Does the content work? Is it organized effectively? Do the text, visuals, and design work together?

Edit next: Improve sentences for clarity, concision, and correctness. Then proofread last for small errors you may have missed.

Fix the document before you polish the details.

If you start with proofreading, you may spend time perfecting something that still needs to be changed, moved, or cut.

Start With the Biggest Problem

Revision doesn’t have to mean working from the first line of your document to the last.

Instead, look at the whole learning aid and ask: What one change would improve this document the most?

Revise from big to small:

  • Content: Is anything important missing or unnecessary?
  • Organization: Are the ideas arranged effectively?
  • Design & visuals: Does the page help readers understand and use the information?
  • Language: Can the writing be clearer or more concise?
  • Details: Are there errors to correct?

Fixing a major problem may also solve several smaller ones. Reorganizing a crowded section, for example, may reveal what you can cut and where a visual would work better.

Don’t give every problem equal attention. Start with the change that will make the biggest difference, then work toward the details.
