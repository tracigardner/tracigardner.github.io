🤔Your Reader Has a Decision to Make

One of the biggest differences between academic writing and workplace writing is what happens after someone reads it.

🚌 In school, you may write primarily to demonstrate what you know.

🏢 In the workplace, readers usually need information to do something: solve a problem, choose a product, complete a task, or make a decision.

That distinction matters for your Correspondence & Informational Report. Luci Lee and her team will use your memo to decide which accessibility tools might work for their organization.

Every part of your memo should help them understand the tools, their capabilities and limitations, and meaningful differences between them. If information doesn’t help, consider whether it belongs in the report.

Technical writing isn’t just about providing information. It’s about providing the right information for a reader who needs it.

📣Don’t Be a Cheerleader

Imagine that you’re comparing two tools and discover that you really like one of them. It’s tempting to write: Tool A is clearly the better option.

There’s just one problem: that’s not your job in your first major project.

⚖️ You’re writing an informational report, not a recommendation report. Your job is to give Luci Lee and her team reliable information they can use to make their own decision.

Watch for words that reveal your opinion, such as best, excellent, superior, impressive, easier, and better. Phrases such as I think, I recommend, and you should also shift the writing from informing to persuading.

Instead, describe what you can document:

Tool A checks PDF heading structure and reading order. Tool B checks those features and also identifies color-contrast issues.

Objective writing doesn’t mean avoiding comparisons. It means making comparisons with specific, verifiable information instead of personal judgments.
