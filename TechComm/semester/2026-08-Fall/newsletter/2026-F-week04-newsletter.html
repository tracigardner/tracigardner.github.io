📢If Everything Stands Out, Nothing Stands Out

Bold type! Bright colors! Big headings! Boxes! Highlights! MORE EMPHASIS!

👀 It’s tempting to make important information stand out by emphasizing everything that seems important.

🎯 But visual hierarchy depends on differences. Readers notice what is larger, bolder, brighter, or separated from everything around it.

If too many elements compete for attention, readers have to figure out for themselves what matters most.

Instead, decide what readers should notice first, second, and third. Use size, weight, placement, contrast, and white space to create that reading order.

Good document design doesn’t emphasize everything. It helps readers see what matters most.

🪧A Heading Is a Promise

Headings do more than break a document into sections. They tell readers what they’ll find if they keep reading.

🔍 A vague heading like Information or Details doesn’t give readers much help.

🧭 A descriptive heading like How the Tool Checks Color Contrast tells readers exactly where they are and what comes next.

That matters in user documents because readers often scan for the information they need instead of reading every word from beginning to end.

As you draft, look at each heading by itself and ask: Would my reader know what information comes next?

Think of every heading as a promise to your reader. Then make sure the section underneath keeps that promise.

🖼️Don’t Just Explain It—Show It

Technical writers use words to explain complicated things—but sometimes words aren’t the best tool for the job.

⚙️ If readers need to understand the parts of an object, recognize something on a screen, compare information, or follow a process, a visual may communicate it faster.

💡 Consider whether a labeled image, diagram, table, screenshot, or other visual could replace—or clarify—a paragraph of explanation.

But don’t add visuals just to make a document look interesting. A useful visual should help readers understand, locate, compare, or do something.

Ask yourself: What could my reader understand faster if I showed it instead of only describing it?

In a user document, visuals aren’t decoration. They’re part of the explanation.

🔬A Description Is More Than a List of Parts

A technical description explains an object, tool, technology, or system so readers can understand what it is and how it works.

🧩 That means describing its parts and their relationships—not simply listing features one after another.

🔗 Help readers see the whole first. Identify the thing and its purpose, then organize the details logically—perhaps outside to inside, top to bottom, part to part, or by how the system operates.

And remember your audience. Include enough technical detail to understand the subject, but not every detail you can possibly find.

A good technical description helps readers build a clear mental picture of the whole.
