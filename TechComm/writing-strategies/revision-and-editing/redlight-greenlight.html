Red Light, Green Light

Is Your User Document Ready?

Give your document six quick tests before you submit. A red light means stop and revise. A green light means the document is meeting the goal.

Useful?

Can readers understand the concepts and use them?

Red Light — Revise

Information is incomplete, unclear, or mostly definitions without practical application.

Green Light — Go

Information is accurate, relevant, appropriately detailed, and helps readers apply the concepts.

Visual?

Do the visuals help communicate the ideas?

Red Light — Revise

Visuals are decorative, unclear, poorly labeled, or disconnected from the explanation.

Green Light — Go

Visuals and text work together to help readers understand and apply the concepts.

Scannable?

Can readers find what they need quickly?

Red Light — Revise

The page feels dense, important information gets lost, or related information is difficult to locate.

Green Light — Go

Clear hierarchy, grouping, alignment, typography, and white space make the page easy to scan.

Reader-Centered?

Does this work for a non-expert employee?

Red Light — Revise

The document assumes too much knowledge or gives readers too little guidance.

Green Light — Go

Explanations, examples, and presentation anticipate what readers need to understand or do.

Clear?

Is the language easy to understand?

Red Light — Revise

Wordiness, jargon, vague language, dense text, or complicated sentences interfere with understanding.

Green Light — Go

Writing is concise, concrete, reader-friendly, and effectively chunked.

Accessible?

Can different readers comfortably read and use it?

Red Light — Revise

Font size, typography, contrast, alignment, or visual presentation may make the page difficult to use.

Green Light — Go

Typography, contrast, alignment, and visual presentation support readability and access.

Stop

Before You Go: Check the Basics

Quality checks come after the assignment’s minimum requirements.

One page, front only.
A coherent group of related concepts.
A meaningful visual component.
Appropriate for the assigned audience and task.
Borrowed information and visuals documented.
Saved as a PDF for submission.
