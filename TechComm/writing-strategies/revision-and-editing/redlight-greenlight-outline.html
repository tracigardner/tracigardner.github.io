Red Light, Green Light

Is Your Outline Ready?

Give your formal working outline six quick tests before submitting it. A red light means stop and revise. A green light means the outline is meeting the goal.

Organized?

Does the outline follow the required report structure and sequence?

Red Light — Revise

Required report elements are missing or out of order, or optional elements are added without a purpose.

Green Light — Go

All required report elements appear in the assigned order; optional elements appear only when useful. The organization leads readers from investigation to recommendations.

Specific?

Can readers tell what you will examine in your client document?

Red Light — Revise

The outline lists only generic labels such as Methods, Results, and Recommendations, with little indication of the actual investigation.

Green Light — Go

A working title identifies the client document and report purpose; headings and subpoints identify the particular problems, criteria, evidence, and improvements you plan to address.

Divided Correctly?

Does every subdivided point have at least two parts?

Red Light — Revise

A point has only one subpoint—an A without a B or a 1 without a 2.

Green Light — Go

Each subdivision has at least two related items; points that do not need subdividing remain at the higher level.

Parallel?

Are related points consistent in importance and wording?

Red Light — Revise

Sibling points mix major topics with minor details or use inconsistent grammatical forms; headings for related topics change unpredictably across sections.

Green Light — Go

Points at the same level are comparable and parallel. Related topics are named consistently across sections—for example, Methods and Results.

Connected?

Can readers trace a path from evaluation criteria to recommendations?

Red Light — Revise

Supporting details sit under unrelated headings, or findings and recommendations seem disconnected from the planned evaluation.

Green Light — Go

Each detail develops its parent point, and the plan connects methods and criteria to results, conclusions, and feasible recommendations.

Easy to Follow?

Can someone see the plan without reading full paragraphs?

Red Light — Revise

Inconsistent numbering or indentation hides the hierarchy, or long passages read like a draft rather than a working outline.

Green Light — Go

Roman numerals, capital letters, Arabic numerals, and lowercase letters show levels clearly; concise phrases or brief notes make the plan easy to scan.

Stop

Before You Go: Check the Basics

Check the assignment requirements and submission details before you go.

A working report title identifies the client document and the report’s purpose.
All required report sections appear in the order specified by the Recommendation Report Assignment.
Optional lists, glossary, or appendices appear only if needed.
Headings and subheadings identify client-specific topics, criteria, evidence, and planned improvements.
Outline levels follow I, A, 1, a with consistent indentation; each subdivided point has at least two parts.
The outline is about 1–2 pages (longer is okay); it uses concise phrases or notes.
