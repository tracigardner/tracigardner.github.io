The Reverse Outline

Does Your Proposal Make Its Case?

You already wrote the proposal. Now uncover the outline hiding inside it. A reverse outline reduces each paragraph to its main job so you can see gaps, repetition, misplaced information, and weak organization.

Revise the Structure Before the Sentences

Before you polish wording, make sure the proposal gives the Project Lead a clear reason to approve your investigation.

1

Label Every Paragraph

Write a 3–7 word note that names what the paragraph does.

Read one paragraph at a time. In the margin, write a short label for its rhetorical job.

What story does your proposal tell when you read only the paragraph jobs?

Give the Reverse Outline Five Tests

One Job?

Does each paragraph have one clear purpose?

Watch for: one paragraph that explains the problem, discusses research, and describes the schedule.

Revise: Split paragraphs that are trying to do unrelated jobs.

Together?

Do paragraphs doing related work stay together?

Watch for:

Problem→ Method→ Problem again

Revise: Group related work so readers do not have to reconstruct the logic.

Repeated?

Are you making the same point more than once?

Watch for: several labels that say some version of “why the document needs investigation.”

Revise: Combine repeated ideas or keep the point only where it does the most useful work.

Missing?

Does the Project Lead get what is needed to approve the project?

What document am I investigating?
Why does it warrant investigation?
How will I investigate it?
Who needs the eventual recommendations?
Is my plan and timetable realistic?

If you cannot point to a paragraph that does the job, you have found a gap.

Builds the Case?

Does the sequence give the reader a sensible path toward approval?

Here’s the document→ Here’s the problem→ Here’s why it matters→ Here’s how I’ll investigate→ Here’s the evidence I’ll use→ Here’s why the plan is realistic

The goal is not one required paragraph order. The goal is a sequence that makes sense to the Project Lead.

Reverse outlining helps you revise the proposal at the paragraph level before you move into sentence-level editing and proofreading.
