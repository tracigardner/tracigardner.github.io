The Reverse Outline

Does Your Proposal Make Its Case?

You wrote the proposal. Now let AI show you the outline it sees. Then verify, question, and revise that map so the proposal makes a clear case for approval.

Use AI as a Reader, Not an Editor

A reverse outline shows the job each paragraph actually performs. AI can create the first-pass map. You decide whether the map accurately represents the proposal you meant to write.

1

Ask AI to Map Your Draft

Have AI label the main job of every paragraph without revising your writing.

Use This Prompt

Create a reverse outline of my proposal. For each paragraph, write a 3–7 word phrase that describes the paragraph’s main job.

  • Number the labels so I can match them to my paragraphs.
  • Describe what each paragraph does, not simply what it is about.
  • Do not revise my writing or suggest improvements.
  • Do not combine paragraphs.
  • Base the labels only on what the draft actually says.
Too Vague Accessibility
Names the Job Explains accessibility criteria
2

Check the AI’s Map

Put each AI label beside its paragraph and compare the label with what the paragraph actually does.

MATCH

The label accurately describes the paragraph’s main job. The paragraph seems to be doing what you intended.

MUDDY

The label is partly right, but something does not fit. The paragraph may mix ideas, do too much, or hide its purpose.

MISMATCH

The label does not describe the job you intended. Investigate why a reader might understand the paragraph differently.

A surprising AI label is a reason to investigate the paragraph—not proof that the AI is correct. Do not simply rewrite the label until the outline looks better.
3

Read the Outline—Not the Proposal

After you verify or correct the labels, ignore the prose and inspect the sequence.

Identifies document
Describes communication problem
Explains why the problem matters
Introduces evaluation plan
Describes usability analysis
Describes accessibility analysis
Establishes qualifications
Shows project schedule

What story does your proposal tell when you read only the paragraph jobs?

Give the Reverse Outline Five Tests

One Job?

Does each paragraph have one clear purpose?

Watch for: an AI label that needs several jobs, such as “explains problem + discusses research + describes schedule.”

Revise: Split or refocus paragraphs that are trying to do unrelated work.

Together?

Do paragraphs doing related work stay together?

Watch for:

Problem→ Method→ Problem again

Revise: Move paragraphs when another sequence would make their relationship clearer.

Repeated?

Are several paragraphs doing the same job?

Watch for: several labels that say some version of “explains why investigation is needed.”

Revise: Combine repeated ideas, distinguish their purposes, or keep the point only where it does the most useful work.

Missing?

Does the Project Lead get what is needed to approve the project?

What document am I investigating?
Why does it warrant investigation?
How will I research and analyze it?
Who needs the eventual recommendations?
Is my plan and timetable realistic?

If you cannot point to where the proposal does one of these jobs, you may have found a gap.

Builds the Case?

Does the sequence help the Project Lead understand and evaluate your proposal?

Here’s the document→ Here’s the problem→ Here’s why it matters→ Here’s how I’ll investigate→ Here’s the evidence I’ll use→ Here’s why the plan is realistic

The goal is not one required paragraph order. The goal is a sequence that makes sense to the Project Lead and supports your request for approval.

Step 4: Revise the Proposal—Not Just the Outline

The AI-generated map is a diagnostic tool. Your draft is what needs revision.

MOVE paragraphs that are out of place.
COMBINE paragraphs that repeat the same job.
SPLIT paragraphs trying to do too much.
ADD information the Project Lead needs but cannot find.
DELETE material that does not contribute to the proposal.
REFOCUS paragraphs whose purpose is unclear.

Then test again: Ask AI for a new reverse outline and see whether the structure is clearer. You are checking the revision—not trying to make the AI’s answer look right.

AI Maps the Structure It Sees. You Decide What Needs to Change.

Check the map → diagnose the draft → revise the structure → then edit the sentences.
