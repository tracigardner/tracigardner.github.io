The 5-Second Test

What Can a Reader Find?

Look at your document for five seconds. Don’t read closely—just scan. Then look away and ask what you noticed.

Try This 5-Second Test

Step 1: Look

Scan the page for five seconds.

Step 2: Look Away

Stop looking at the document.

Step 3: Assess

What was clear? What was hard to find?

Step 4: Revise

Fix anything readers may miss.

What Did You Find?

If you answer no to any question, use the revision advice directly below it.

1

What Is This About?

Can you quickly identify the main topic and purpose?

If not, strengthen the title and opening message. Make the topic specific and tell readers what the learning aid will help them understand or do.
2

What Matters Most?

Can you tell which information is most important?

If not, strengthen the visual hierarchy. Use size, weight, contrast, placement, and white space to make key information stand out.
3

Where Would You Start?

Do headings and visual cues give you an obvious entry point?

If not, improve headings and navigation. Use descriptive headings and clear visual cues to guide readers through the page.
4

What Belongs Together?

Can you see which ideas are related without reading every word?

If not, improve grouping. Use proximity, alignment, repetition, and white space to keep related information together and separate different ideas.
5

What Can You Do With It?

Can you tell how to use or apply the information?

If not, strengthen the connection between explanation and application. Use meaningful visuals, examples, labels, or brief guidance to show what the concepts look like and how to use them.
