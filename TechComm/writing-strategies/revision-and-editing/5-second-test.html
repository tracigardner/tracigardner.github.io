The 5-Second Test

What Can a Reader Find?

Look at your document for five seconds. Don’t read closely—just scan. Then look away and ask what you noticed.

How to Do the 5-Second Test

Step 1 Look

Scan the page for five seconds.

Step 2 Look Away

Stop looking at the document.

Step 3 Assess

What was clear? What was hard to find?

Step 4 Revise

Fix anything readers may miss.

Ask Yourself These Questions

1

What Is This About?

Can I quickly identify the main topic and purpose?

2

What Matters Most?

Is the key information easy to spot?

3

Where Would I Start?

Do headings and visual cues guide me to the right place?

4

How Is the Information Organized?

Can I see which ideas belong together?

5

What Can I Do With This Information?

Is it clear how the ideas can be used or applied?

Quick Checklist

Use these checks to see if your document is easy to scan.

Title: Is the topic clear and specific?
Headings: Do they show the structure and help readers find information quickly?
Hierarchy: Does the most important information stand out?
Grouping: Are related ideas placed together?
Emphasis: Are key points easy to find?
Visuals: Do visuals help explain or apply the information?
White Space: Is there enough space to make the page easy to scan?

The Goal

A reader should be able to understand what your document is about, find the most important information, and see how to use it—all in five seconds.

Design for the reader, not the page.
