Academic Writing vs.
Technical Communication

Different goals. Different audiences. Different approaches.

Academic Writing

Writing to demonstrate
what you know.

FROM “WHAT I KNOW” TO “WHAT YOU NEED”

Technical Communication

Designing information so
someone can use it.

Demonstrates knowledge, develops an argument, or contributes to scholarly discussion.
Purpose
Helps readers understand information, make decisions, or take action.
Usually instructors, students, or scholars.
Audience
Clients, coworkers, managers, specialists, users, or the public.
Demonstrates understanding and builds a well-supported interpretation or argument.
Writer’s Role
Provides information readers need to accomplish a specific goal.
Often follows conventional essay or disciplinary structures.
Organization
Designed around readers’ needs and how they will use the document.
Often intended for sustained, beginning-to-end reading.
Reading Pattern
Often designed for scanning, skimming, and selective reading.
Emphasizes scholarly research, analysis, and citation.
Evidence
Uses the evidence most useful and credible for the situation, including research, data, standards, testing, and expert knowledge.
Often develops ideas through extended explanation and analysis.
Style
Prioritizes clarity, concision, precision, and usability.
Formatting often follows established academic conventions. Visuals usually supplement the written discussion.
Design & Visuals
Headings, lists, tables, graphics, white space, and typography help communicate information. Visuals can carry essential information.
The reader understands and accepts the writer’s analysis or argument.
Success Means
The reader can find, understand, and use the information effectively.

The Big Takeaway

Academic writing
shows what you know.

Technical communication
focuses on what your reader needs.
