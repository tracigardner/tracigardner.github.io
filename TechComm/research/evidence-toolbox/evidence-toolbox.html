Build Your Investigation

Evidence Toolbox

Different Questions Call for Different Evidence

A strong research plan combines what you can learn by examining the client document itself with what you can learn from credible outside sources. Start with the communication problem you want to investigate, then choose evidence that will help you understand and evaluate it.

Open the Evidence Toolbox

Look Beyond the Document

Use outside evidence to establish what effective communication should do.

Standards & Guidelines

Accessibility standards, plain-language guidance, and document-design recommendations.

Professional Sources

Disciplinary, industry, and professional guidance relevant to the document or audience.

Comparison Documents

Similar documents that show alternative ways to organize, design, or present information.

Usability Principles

Research and guidance about reader tasks, navigation, information finding, and ease of use.

Credible Research

Evidence that helps explain why a communication problem matters and what effective practice looks like.

Look at the Document

Generate evidence by examining what the client document actually does.

Organization

Examine headings, structure, grouping, information flow, and navigation.

Design

Consider layout, visual hierarchy, typography, graphics, spacing, and readability.

Language

Look at clarity, tone, wording, sentence structure, concision, and plain-language issues.

Accessibility

Examine contrast, heading structure, alternative text, navigation, and other accessibility features.

Users

Consider who uses the document and what those readers need to find, understand, or do.

The Right Evidence Leads to Stronger Recommendations

From Evidence to Recommendations

1

Observe

Identify what the client document currently does and where communication problems may exist.

2

Research

Find credible standards, guidance, research, and examples relevant to the issue.

3

Evaluate

Compare what the document does with what credible evidence says effective communication should do.

4

Recommend

Use the evidence to develop specific, defensible improvements for the client.

Show What You Will Actually Investigate

Too General

“I will research accessibility, usability, and document design.”

This lists topics, but it does not explain what you will examine or what evidence you will use.

More Investigative

“I will analyze the document’s navigation and heading structure, consult usability and plain-language guidance, and compare the document’s current design with those recommendations.”

This identifies specific actions and sources of evidence.

Choose the Evidence That Fits the Problem

You do not need every tool in the toolbox. Identify what you need to investigate, then choose the methods and sources that will help you answer those questions.
