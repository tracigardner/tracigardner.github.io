Proposal Structure

Section 1: Memo Headers

Begin with standard memo headers:

To: [Caldwell supervisor]
From: [Your name], Consultant, Caldwell & Associates
Date: [Submission date]
Subject: Proposal to Evaluate [client document]

Make the subject line specific. Do not add a heading labeled “Memo Headers.”

Section 2: Introduction

In a brief opening paragraph, identify the client organization, the document you propose to analyze, its general purpose and audience, and your request for approval to proceed. Do not add an “Introduction” heading.

Section 3: Overview and Rationale

Briefly describe the document, what it is intended to accomplish, and how its audience uses it. Then explain why it is worth investigating.

Identify preliminary concerns or questions involving document design and usability, plain language, and accessibility. Use specific evidence from the document, but treat these concerns as areas for investigation rather than final conclusions.

Section 4: Methods and Resources

Explain how you will analyze the document and research appropriate communication standards. Your plan should address:

  • document design and usability;
  • plain language;
  • accessibility; and
  • authoritative standards, guidelines, or professional recommendations.

Identify specific resources you expect to consult when possible, such as government guidance, professional organizations, accessibility standards, plain-language guidance, usability research, or disciplinary standards. Show that you know where to look for reliable evidence.

Section 5: Project Audience

Analyze the audience for the recommendation report. Identify the people within the client organization who could evaluate or implement your recommendations.

Consider:

  • what they already know;
  • what they need from Caldwell;
  • what constraints may affect their decisions; and
  • how they will use the final report.

Also consider the users of the client document, since their needs should shape your eventual recommendations.

Section 6: Personnel & Qualifications

Identify yourself as the Caldwell consultant responsible for the project and briefly explain the experience or skills that prepare you to complete the work. Relevant qualifications might include coursework, disciplinary knowledge, research experience, workplace experience, or skills in technical communication, document design, plain language, or accessibility.

You do not need to be an expert in the client’s field. Explain how your existing knowledge and research skills prepare you for the project. Do not invent credentials.

Section 7: Timetable

Provide a table showing the major stages of your work and when you expect to complete them. Include:

  • document review;
  • research;
  • analysis;
  • drafting; and
  • revision and submission.

Use actual course deadlines when available and allow enough time for research, analysis, drafting, and revision.

Section 8: Conclusion

End with a short paragraph that reinforces the value of the project and directly requests approval to proceed. Do not introduce new information, and do not add a “Conclusion” heading.
