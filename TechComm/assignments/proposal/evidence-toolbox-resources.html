Evidence Toolbox

Accessibility

Use trusted standards and guidance to evaluate how well a document works for people with different abilities and technologies.

Start Here When You Need to Ask:

Use accessibility resources when your investigation asks questions such as:

  • Does the document meet established accessibility expectations?
  • What barriers might some users encounter?
  • What specific criteria can support an accessibility recommendation?
W3C

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2

The W3C standard provides specific success criteria for accessible web content.

Use It For

  • Finding specific accessibility criteria.
  • Checking content, structure, contrast, and interaction.
  • Supporting recommendations with an established standard.
Visit WCAG 2.2
W3C WAI

WAI Tutorials

W3C’s Web Accessibility Initiative explains how accessibility principles apply to common content and design tasks.

Use It For

  • Seeing practical accessibility examples.
  • Investigating images, tables, structure, forms, and menus.
  • Understanding how WCAG can be applied in practice.
Explore WAI Tutorials
Section 508.gov

ICT Testing Baselines

Section508.gov provides federal testing baselines for evaluating accessibility requirements in information and communication technology.

Use It For

  • Finding structured accessibility testing criteria.
  • Checking electronic documents and digital content.
  • Understanding federal accessibility expectations.
View Section 508 Baselines

Standards Give You Criteria

Do not simply label something “inaccessible.” Use a standard or guideline to identify the feature you will examine and explain why it matters.

Plain Language

Use established guidance and assessment tools to evaluate whether readers can find, understand, and use the information they need.

Start Here When You Need to Ask:

Use plain-language resources when your investigation asks questions such as:

  • Is the document organized around what readers need?
  • Are wording, sentences, headings, and layout easy to understand?
  • Can readers quickly identify the main message or required action?
Digital.gov

Plain Language Guide

Digital.gov explains how to write, design, and test content so a specific audience can understand it.

Use It For

  • Evaluating audience focus and organization.
  • Checking clear wording and reader-centered writing.
  • Examining how design supports understanding.
Explore the Plain Language Guide
CDC

Clear Communication Index

The CDC Index is a research-based tool for developing and assessing public communication materials.

Use It For

  • Evaluating the main message and call to action.
  • Checking plain language and information design.
  • Using a structured assessment rather than intuition alone.
Use the CDC Index
National Archives

Plain Writing Checklist

The National Archives checklist provides a concise review of audience, organization, wording, sentences, headings, lists, tables, and white space.

Use It For

  • Running a quick plain-language review.
  • Checking organization and sentence-level clarity.
  • Evaluating readability features such as headings and white space.
Open the Checklist

Plain Language Is More Than Short Words

Evaluate the whole communication experience: audience, organization, main message, wording, headings, information design, and the actions readers need to take.

Evidence Toolbox

Usability

Use research-based principles and user-centered methods to evaluate whether people can find information, understand what to do, and complete their tasks successfully.

Start Here When You Need to Ask:

Use usability resources when your investigation asks questions such as:

  • Can users find what they need without unnecessary effort?
  • Does the document match users’ expectations and tasks?
  • What would you need to observe or test with actual users?
NN/g

10 Usability Heuristics

Jakob Nielsen’s heuristics provide broad, established principles for identifying usability problems.

Use It For

  • Checking consistency and user expectations.
  • Evaluating efficiency, clarity, and unnecessary complexity.
  • Conducting a heuristic review of an interactive document or interface.
Explore the 10 Heuristics
USWDS

U.S. Web Design System: Design Principles

USWDS provides user-centered principles intended to serve as an evaluative lens for design and implementation decisions.

Use It For

  • Keeping real user needs at the center of the analysis.
  • Checking accessibility, continuity, trust, and simplicity.
  • Comparing a design with established public-service practices.
Review USWDS Principles
GOV.UK

Moderated Usability Testing

The GOV.UK Service Manual explains how observing people complete realistic tasks can reveal problems with language, layout, and interaction.

Use It For

  • Understanding what usability testing actually observes.
  • Planning task-based evaluation with users.
  • Identifying problems that document inspection alone may miss.
Read the Testing Guide

Usability Is About What People Can Do

A document can look polished and still be difficult to use. Focus on users’ tasks, expectations, navigation, decisions, and ability to complete what they came to do.
