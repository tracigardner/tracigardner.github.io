From Proposal to Recommendation

Follow the work from question to evidence-based action

A Proposal and a Recommendation Report Do Different Jobs

A proposal starts the investigation. A recommendation report comes later, after you have gathered and analyzed evidence. The key is not to jump from noticing a possible problem directly to prescribing a solution.

1

Notice

Spot a Possible Problem

Begin with something you observe in the client document. At this point, you may have a concern, but you do not yet know what the evidence will show.

Example

The guide uses broad headings such as Information and Details.

2

Proposal

Turn the Problem into an Investigation

Explain what you will investigate, why it matters, and how you plan to gather enough evidence to answer the question.

Proposal statement

“I will investigate whether the guide’s heading structure helps users locate procedures.”

Writing Strategies

Evidence available now Preliminary observations + credible outside resources
Typical verbs investigate, examine, evaluate, assess, compare, test
What you ask the reader to do Approve the investigation
3

Investigate

Gather and Analyze Evidence

This is the work between the proposal and the recommendation report. Use the methods and sources you proposed to determine what is actually happening in the document.

Document analysis Standards & guidance Reader testing Comparisons
4

Find

Decide What the Evidence Shows

Findings connect the investigation to the recommendation. They tell you whether the original concern was supported, unsupported, or more complicated than you expected.

Example finding

Readers had difficulty locating procedural sections when the headings did not describe the task beneath them.

5

Recommendation Report

Recommend an Evidence-Based Action

Now you can move from investigation to action because you have evidence to support a specific change.

Recommendation

“Revise the guide’s headings so they describe the procedures beneath them.”

Evidence available now Completed analysis + findings + supporting sources
Typical verbs recommend, revise, add, remove, reorganize, redesign
What you ask the reader to do Consider or implement the recommended changes
