Proposal
for Your Recommendation Report

Your Task

Worth 25% of your course grade

Write a proposal memo requesting approval to investigate a client document and prepare a recommendation report for the organization that created it.

You work as a consultant at Maroon + Orange Digital. Each consultant has been asked to choose a document and propose a plan for evaluating it and developing recommendations for the client.

Review Markel & Selber Ch 8 and 16 for help with this project. Check your work with the Proposal Report Self-Check as you work.

Assignment Learning Objectives

 Hover over terms with dotted underline for more details. 

After working on this assignment, you should be able to:

  • Write a workplace proposal that requests authorization to investigate communication problems in a client document.
  • Define the problem, purpose, scope, audience, and what the proposed project is expected to accomplish.
  • Use credible research, including relevant professional standards and guidelines, to show why the document warrants investigation and support your reasoning.
  • Develop a practical research plan for investigating the communication problem.
  • Develop persuasive content that shows why the project is worthwhile and realistic and why you are qualified to complete it.
  • Include the required proposal sections: introduction, proposed program, qualifications and experience, task schedule, and source documentation.
  • Create an effective Gantt chart that helps readers understand the project’s schedule.
  • Organize, design, write, and revise the proposal for clarity, concision, accessibility, professional tone, and workplace proposal conventions.

Your Document

Ten clients have contacted Maroon + Orange Digital with requests to review documents they have produced and identify issues that reduce their effectiveness.

The clients have staff with the expertise to address the content. They need help evaluating how effectively the documents communicate. Each client is particularly interested in document design and usability, plain language, and accessibility.

Choose one of the ten client documents from the Scenario List as the focus of your proposal and your recommendation report.

Your Audience

Write to the Project Lead at Maroon + Orange Digital, who must approve your project before you begin the recommendation report. The Project Lead is familiar with the client project and has access to the submitted documents.

The Project Lead needs to know:

  • what document you selected and why it warrants investigation.
  • how you will research and analyze it.
  • who needs the eventual recommendations.
  • whether your plan and timetable are realistic.

Keep in mind that three different audiences are important to this project:

  1. The Project Lead is the audience for the proposal.
  2. The client organization is the audience for the recommendation report you are proposing.
  3. The people who use the client document are an additional audience whose needs should guide your evaluation.

Assignment Requirements

Basic Requirements

  • Genre: Workplace-ready proposal memo
  • Length: 4–5 pages, including the Gantt chart. Longer documents okay.
  • Layout: 1" margins all around. Number pages 2 to the end. No page number on the first page.
  • Document Design:
    • Single space within paragraphs.
    • Double space between paragraphs and around headings.
    • Do not indent paragraphs.
    • Use hierarchical headings to structure the body.
  • Plain Language: Use short sentences, chunked paragraphs, and other plain language techniques.

Content & Structure Requirements

  • Required Sections:
    • Memo headers
    • Introduction (no heading)
    • Proposed Program
    • Qualifications and Experience
    • Task Schedule or Proposed Timeline (set up as a Gantt chart)
    • References/Works Cited/Bibliography (use the title required by your field’s documentation style)
  • Content: Follow details in “The Structure of the Proposal,” in Ch 16: Writing Proposals.
  • Not Required: You do not need a Summary or Budget for your proposal.

Visuals Requirements

  • Number: At least one relevant visual aid, in addition to the Gantt chart.
  • Examples: Photographs, tables, charts, diagrams, and other relevant visuals. No clip art.
  • Location: On the same page and alongside or after the reference to it in the text.
  • Caption: A two-part caption—a number and descriptive title—for each visual (for example, Figure 1: Current Navigation Structure, or Table 1: Accessibility Issues to Investigate).
  • Integration: Refer to each visual by number in the text before it appears (for example, “As Figure 1 shows…”).
  • Help: See Ch 12: Creating Graphics.

Research & Documentation Requirements

  • Number: At least three outside sources.
  • Purpose & Use: Use preliminary research to show why the document warrants investigation and identify resources for your evaluation.
  • Sources: Use credible standards, guidelines, research, and professional recommendations relevant to your project.
  • Style: Use your discipline’s documentation style (e.g., engineers use IEEE, biologists use CSE).
  • In-Text Citations: Cite information taken from outside sources, according to your discipline’s documentation style.
  • Full Citations: List all cited sources in your Works Cited, References, or Bibliography at the end of the proposal, according to your discipline’s documentation style.
  • Help: See Documentation and Citations for Your Projects as well as Ch 6: Researching Your Subject and App B: Documenting Your Sources.

Success Tips

  • Choose a document to analyze based on your interests. You are not required to choose one related to your major.
  • Think long-term. You will work with the document you choose for the rest of the semester.
  • Be specific. Refer directly to your selected document and client in your proposal. Make sure your choice is clear.
  • Discuss your research plan. Outline the strategies you will use to analyze the document and make recommendations. You’re explaining your plan to research and develop the Recommendation Report.
  • Follow the textbook explanations and examples. Ch 16: Writing Proposals explains the major parts of a proposal and provides models you can adapt to this project.
