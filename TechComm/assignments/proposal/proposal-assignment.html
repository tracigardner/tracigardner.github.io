Proposal
Recommendation Report Project

Your Task

Worth 25% of your course grade

Write a proposal memo requesting approval to investigate one client document and develop a recommendation report for the organization that created it.

You work as a consultant at Caldwell & Associates. Ten clients have provided documents that offer opportunities to investigate document design and usability,plain language, and accessibility. Choose the document that interests you most. You are not required to choose one connected to your major.

You work as a Consultant at Maroon + Orange Digital, a company that helps their clients improve their communication with customers, colleagues, and the public. See the full scenario for all the details on the writing situation.

Explain what you plan to investigate, why the project is worthwhile, how you will conduct the research and analysis, and what resources you expect to use. Your goal is to show your Caldwell supervisor that you have a worthwhile project and a reasonable plan for completing it.

Review Markel & Selber Ch XXX for help with this project. Check your work with the Short Passage Revision Quiz Report Self-Check as you work.

Assignment Learning Objectives

Hide the Learning Objectives Show the Learning Objectives

After working on this assignment, you should be able to:

  • Write a short workplace proposal that requests authorization to proceed with a project.
  • Define the purpose, scope, audience, and expected outcome of a proposed project.
  • Develop a practical research method for investigating a workplace communication problem.
  • Identify credible standards, guidelines, and other resources for professional research.
  • Describe your qualifications and develop a realistic project schedule.
  • Use headings, tables, and other design features to make a proposal easy to evaluate.

The Larger Project

Your later recommendation report will evaluate how effectively the selected document communicates with its intended audience and recommend standards the client can use in this document and future documents.

  • Document Design & Usability: Can readers find, understand, and use the information effectively?
  • Plain Language: Is the information clear and appropriately complex for the audience?
  • Accessibility: Can people with different abilities and technologies access and use the information?

For the proposal, identify promising areas for investigation rather than presenting your final analysis or recommendations.

Your Audience

Write to your supervisor at Caldwell & Associates, who must approve your project before you begin the recommendation report.

Your supervisor needs to know:

  • what document you selected and why it warrants investigation;
  • how you will research and analyze it;
  • who needs the eventual recommendations; and
  • whether your plan and timetable are realistic.

Your supervisor is the audience for the proposal. The client organization is the audience for the recommendation report you are proposing.

  • Layout: 1" margins all around. Number pages 2 to the end. No page number on the first page.

  • Tone: Professional, confident, specific, and forward-looking.

Section 1: Memo Headers

Begin with standard memo headers:

To: [Caldwell supervisor]
From: [Your name], Consultant, Caldwell & Associates
Date: [Submission date]
Subject: Proposal to Evaluate [client document]

Make the subject line specific. Do not add a heading labeled “Memo Headers.”

Section 2: Introduction

In a brief opening paragraph, identify the client organization, the document you propose to analyze, its general purpose and audience, and your request for approval to proceed. Do not add an “Introduction” heading.

Section 3: Overview and Rationale

Briefly describe the document, what it is intended to accomplish, and how its audience uses it. Then explain why it is worth investigating.

Identify preliminary concerns or questions involving document design and usability, plain language, and accessibility. Use specific evidence from the document, but treat these concerns as areas for investigation rather than final conclusions.

Section 4: Methods and Resources

Explain how you will analyze the document and research appropriate communication standards. Your plan should address:

  • document design and usability;
  • plain language;
  • accessibility; and
  • authoritative standards, guidelines, or professional recommendations.

Identify specific resources you expect to consult when possible, such as government guidance, professional organizations, accessibility standards, plain-language guidance, usability research, or disciplinary standards. Show that you know where to look for reliable evidence.

Section 5: Project Audience

Analyze the audience for the recommendation report. Identify the people within the client organization who could evaluate or implement your recommendations.

Consider:

  • what they already know;
  • what they need from Caldwell;
  • what constraints may affect their decisions; and
  • how they will use the final report.

Also consider the users of the client document, since their needs should shape your eventual recommendations.

Section 6: Personnel & Qualifications

Identify yourself as the Caldwell consultant responsible for the project and briefly explain the experience or skills that prepare you to complete the work. Relevant qualifications might include coursework, disciplinary knowledge, research experience, workplace experience, or skills in technical communication, document design, plain language, or accessibility.

You do not need to be an expert in the client’s field. Explain how your existing knowledge and research skills prepare you for the project. Do not invent credentials.

Section 7: Timetable

Provide a table showing the major stages of your work and when you expect to complete them. Include:

  • document review;
  • research;
  • analysis;
  • drafting; and
  • revision and submission.

Use actual course deadlines when available and allow enough time for research, analysis, drafting, and revision.

Section 8: Conclusion

End with a short paragraph that reinforces the value of the project and directly requests approval to proceed. Do not introduce new information, and do not add a “Conclusion” heading.

Success Tips

  • Choose based on interest, not your major. All ten options support the same recommendation-report assignment.
  • Be specific. This assignment page is generic; your proposal should refer directly to your selected document and client.
  • Propose an investigation. Identify preliminary concerns without turning the proposal into the final recommendation report.
  • Think beyond individual corrections. Your eventual recommendations should help the client improve future documents as well as the one you analyze.
