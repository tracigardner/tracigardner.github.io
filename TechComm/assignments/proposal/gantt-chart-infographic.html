Gantt Charts

Show the Work, Not Just the Dates

Your proposal’s Gantt chart should show how you will complete the investigation—not simply when you plan to research, write, and revise.

Think of the Gantt Chart as a Visual Research Plan

The Project Lead should be able to look at your chart and understand the major tasks you will complete, when they will happen, how long they will take, and where parts of the project overlap.

Which Chart Shows the Better Plan?

Too General

The dates are visible, but the actual investigation is not.

Task Week 1 Week 2 Week 3 Week 4
Research Scheduled Scheduled
Write Report Scheduled Scheduled
Revise Scheduled

Why It Falls Short

  • “Research” hides several different kinds of work.
  • The chart does not show how the document will actually be evaluated.
  • The reader cannot tell what evidence will support the recommendations.
  • The schedule looks more like a homework calendar than a project plan.

Specific and Investigative

The schedule reveals the work behind the recommendation report.

Task Week 1 Week 2 Week 3 Week 4
Review Client Document Scheduled
Research Standards & Guidelines Scheduled Scheduled
Evaluate Design & Usability Scheduled Scheduled
Evaluate Plain Language & Accessibility Scheduled Scheduled
Develop Recommendations Scheduled Scheduled
Draft & Revise Report Scheduled Scheduled

Why It Works

  • Tasks describe specific project activities.
  • The chart shows how the client document will be investigated.
  • Research and analysis connect visibly to later recommendations.
  • Overlapping tasks show a realistic working process.

Build a Gantt Chart That Explains Your Plan

1

Name the Actual Tasks

Replace vague labels such as research with the specific activities you will complete.

2

Show the Sequence

Make it clear what needs to happen first and which later work depends on earlier investigation.

3

Show Duration

Use bars across the timeline to show approximately how long each task will take.

4

Show Realistic Overlap

Research, analysis, writing, and revision do not always happen one at a time. Show overlap when it reflects your actual plan.

Make Your Task Labels Do More Work

A useful task name tells the Project Lead what you will actually do.

Too Vague

  • Research
  • Analyze
  • Write
  • Revise

More Informative

  • Review the client document and identify communication problems.
  • Research relevant professional standards and guidelines.
  • Evaluate document design and usability.
  • Evaluate plain language and accessibility.
  • Organize findings and develop recommendations.
  • Draft, test, and revise the recommendation report.

The 5-Second Gantt Test

Look at your chart without reading the rest of the proposal. Can you tell what you will investigate, how the work unfolds, and when major tasks will happen? If not, make the task labels more specific.
