Assignment Requirements

Basic Requirements

  • Length: 1 page (front only).
  • Documentation: If you use any information from outside sources (definitions, procedures, diagrams), cite them in-text and at the end of the document using your field’s documentation style.

  • Plain Language: Use short sentences and other plain language techniques (like active voice, chunked paragraphs, and concrete words). Avoid jargon.
  • Tone: Helpful, clear, and informative—avoid academic or formal language. Write as if you are standing beside the user, guiding them personally.
  • Document Design: Follow principles of effective document design (CRAP: contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity).

User Document Requirements

  • Design your user document for clarity and ease of use.
  • Use headings/subheadings, numbered/bulleted lists, and white space for navigation (so readers can find the information they need quickly).
  • Include visuals alongside explanatory text to support understanding and organize the document. See examples section.

Formatting Requirements

  • Page Size: 8.5" x 11" (portrait orientation).
  • Margins: .25" margin, as your document will not be printed.
  • Spacing: Single or 1.15 spacing between lines; double spacing between paragraphs (1 blank line).
  • Colors: Full bleed (background color covers the entire page, or, in other words, bleed off the page) or white background; high contrast color choices (text and images visible against background).
  • Paragraphs: Do not indent.
  • Pagination: None. Not needed for one-page documents.
  • Font Choices: Arial, Calibri, Verdana, Aptos, or Helvetica—but just one! Don’t mix fonts.
  • Hierarchical Headings: Headings should be larger than the body text. Each heading level should be larger than the next lower heading (e.g., Heading 1 is larger than Heading 2; Heading 2 is larger than Heading 3, and so on).

    TIP: The default heading sizes in Word and Google Docs follow this guideline automatically.

Success Tips

  • Ask a friend to read your document. If they get confused, revise for clarity.
  • Break your ideas into short, manageable segments (chunks) so readers never face a wall of text.
  • Combine concise explanations with examples, visuals, comparisons, annotations, or other design elements that make the information easier to understand and use.
  • Test your document with your word processor’s accessibility review tools to ensure that it’s design is easy for your audience to read. In Word, go to the Review Ribbon, and choose the Check Accessibility button.
