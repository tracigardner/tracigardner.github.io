Assignment Learning Objectives

By completing this assignment, you will develop your ability to:

  • Translate specialized or technical information into a clear, helpful document that supports real-world action or decision-making by a non-expert audience.
  • Design a usable, accessible document that meets the needs of a specific audience by applying plain language principles, strong visual communication strategies, and effective document design (CRAP: contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity).
  • Choose the most appropriate user-facing document structures based on your topic and the needs of your audience.
  • Demonstrate audience awareness by tailoring your tone, structure, and content to support users in understanding, navigating, or acting on a complex subject with ease and confidence.

Your Audience

Write your project for non-expert users—such as new users, community members, caregivers, students, or volunteers. These non-experts should be specific people from the local community you have chosen.

They should be able to use the document to better understand, use, decide about, or troubleshoot the subject you’ve been researching.

Assignment Requirements

Basic Requirements

  • Length: 1 page for a brief cover memo (see below) + 2–3 pages for the user doc itself.
  • Project Order: Place the documents in your file in this order:
    1. Your Cover Memo
    2. Your Step-by-Step Instructions
  • Documentation: If you use any information from outside sources (definitions, procedures, diagrams), cite them in-text and at the end of the document using your field’s documentation style.

  • Plain Language: Use short sentences and other plain language techniques (like active voice and concrete words). Avoid jargon.
  • Tone: Helpful, clear, and informative—avoid academic or formal language. Write as if you are standing beside the user, guiding them personally.
  • Document Design: Follow principles of effective document design (CRAP: contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity).

User Document Requirements

Focus on usability and accessibility. Your user document should be designed for clarity and ease of use. It must:

  • Follow the document structure guidance from 11b. Components of the User Document of the course textbook. Required elements:
    1. title
    2. date
    3. introduction
    4. notices
    5. technical background
    6. equipment & supplies
    7. directions
    8. conclusion
    9. troubleshooting
    10. references (if needed)
  • Use headings/subheadings, numbered/bulleted lists, and white space for navigation.
  • Define any unfamiliar terms for your audience.
  • Include visuals to support understanding, such as diagrams, screenshots, flowcharts, or illustrations:
    • Use at least 4 useful and necessary visuals (diagrams, tables, icons, or screenshots; no clip art unless it serves a purpose).
    • Give each visual a two-part caption (Figure 1: Description of Figure 1).
    • Refer directly to the visual in the text before you show it (See Figure 1, as Figure 1 notes, etc.).
  • Be well-organized and scannable: readers should be able to find the information they need quickly.

Formatting Requirements

  • Page Size: 8.5" x 11" (portrait orientation)
  • Spacing: Single or 1.15 spacing between lines; double spacing between paragraphs (1 blank line)
  • Paragraphs: Do not indent.
  • Pagination: Upper right or bottom right.
  • Font Choices: Arial, Calibri, Verdana, Aptos, or Helvetica—but just one! Don’t mix fonts.
  • Hierarchical Headings: Headings should be larger than the body text. Each heading level should be larger than the next lower heading (e.g., Heading 1 is larger than Heading 2; Heading 2 is larger than Heading 3, and so on).

    TIP: The default heading sizes in Word and Google Docs follow this guideline automatically.

Success Tips

Your learning aid should help readers understand the concepts and apply them to their own work. Rather than simply providing dictionary-style definitions, the document should combine concise explanations with examples, visuals, comparisons, annotations, or other design elements that make the information easier to understand and use.

  • Ask a friend outside your major to read and try your document. If they get confused, revise for clarity.
  • Break complex steps or explanations into short, manageable segments (chunks) so readers never face a wall of text.
  • Pair each visual with just enough explanation so users don’t need to guess what it means.
  • Make key actions, terms, or warnings stand out at a glance using bold text, icons, or callout boxes.
