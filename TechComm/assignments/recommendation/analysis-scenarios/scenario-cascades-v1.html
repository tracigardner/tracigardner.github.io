Cascades Wildlife & Habitat Assessment Review (Fall 2026)

Project Lead: Morgan Lee

Charge Code: 26-3417

Client Request: The Eastern Divide Ranger District wants an independent communication review of its draft Cascades Wildlife & Habitat / Ecosystem Management Assessment.

The district needs to know whether the document helps managers and specialists quickly understand field conditions, trace evidence, compare interventions, and identify what requires further verification before project scoping.

Client Overview

Client: U.S. Forest Service, Eastern Divide Ranger District, George Washington and Jefferson National Forests

Client Location: Giles County, Virginia

Key Contact: Alex Ramirez, District Planning Coordinator

Background: The Eastern Divide Ranger District manages recreation and natural resources within part of the George Washington and Jefferson National Forests. District staff are responsible for stewardship of the Cascades recreation corridor along Little Stony Creek, where high visitor use, steep terrain, stream proximity, erosion, and wildlife habitat create overlapping maintenance and resource-management concerns.

Document Overview

Document: Wildlife & Habitat / Ecosystem Management Assessment

Purpose: Support trail-maintenance budgeting, management-plan updates, and decisions about low-impact infrastructure and resource-protection actions along Little Stony Creek.

Primary Users: District leadership and program staff who need to identify priorities, justify maintenance requests, update management plans, and decide which proposals are ready for further scoping.

Use Context: Managers may scan the assessment in office or meeting settings for priorities, budget implications, and recommended actions. Program specialists may use it to check the evidence and limitations behind a recommendation before further scoping or field verification.

Why the Document Is Being Reviewed

The draft combines useful field observations, measurements, site IDs, tables, recommendations, and monitoring guidance, but district staff have not approved it for management use. They are concerned that priorities may not be immediately visible, evidence may be difficult to trace across sections, and dense tables or specialized terminology may not work equally well for managers, specialists, and field users. Because the assessment may support FY2027–FY2029 maintenance planning, the district wants the communication reviewed before the document is finalized.

The client wants to ensure that the document communicates effectively with its intended users and supports the organization’s goals. The review should consider opportunities to improve:

  • Document design and organization
  • Usability for the intended audience and context
  • Plain language and clarity
  • Accessibility

Your Role & Objective

As a consultant at Maroon + Orange Digital, you will evaluate the client’s document and identify opportunities to make it more effective for its intended users.

Your proposal will explain why this document is an appropriate focus for your project, outline how you plan to investigate it, and request approval to proceed.

Your later recommendation report will analyze the document in greater detail and recommend specific improvements supported by research and evidence.

Additional Context

The 2026 assessment is based on rapid surveys conducted in May, June, and July. It identifies erosion, stream-bank degradation, wildlife-related visitor behavior, and possible interventions such as drainage repair, hardened creek-access points, raised tread, revegetation, visitor messaging, and temporary closures. The evidence is intentionally limited: the team did not conduct continuous visitor counts, winter or post-major-storm observations, water-quality sampling, formal sensitive-species surveys, engineering analysis, or a complete accessibility review. Recommendations therefore need to distinguish actions that can proceed from those requiring specialist verification or additional study.
