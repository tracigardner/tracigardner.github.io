Client Request: Review the draft of the Cascades Wildlife & Habitat / Ecosystem Management Assessment before it is approved for management use. The district wants to know whether readers can quickly find priorities, follow the evidence, and understand what requires further study.

Client Overview

Client: U.S. Forest Service, Eastern Divide Ranger District, George Washington and Jefferson National Forests

Client Location: Giles County, Virginia

Key Contact: Alex Ramirez, District Planning Coordinator

Background: The district manages recreation and natural resources along the Cascades National Recreation Trail and Little Stony Creek. Heavy use, steep terrain, erosion, stream access, and wildlife habitat create connected maintenance and resource-protection concerns.

Document Overview

Document:
Wildlife & Habitat / Ecosystem Management Assessment
Purpose:
Support maintenance planning, budgeting, and decisions about resource protection along the Cascades corridor.
Primary Users:
District managers, program specialists, and project staff.
Use Context:
Readers need to scan findings, compare priorities, trace recommendations to field evidence, and decide what is ready for further action.

Why the Document Is Being Reviewed

The draft contains useful field observations, measurements, site IDs, tables, recommendations, and monitoring guidance. Staff are concerned that the information may be difficult to scan, connect, and use across different audiences.

The review should consider opportunities to improve:

  • Document design and organization
  • Usability for managers and specialists
  • Plain language and clarity
  • Accessibility

Your Role & Objective

As a consultant at Maroon + Orange Digital, evaluate how well the document helps readers find, understand, and use the existing information.

Your proposal will explain why the document is an appropriate focus and how you plan to investigate it. Your later recommendation report will recommend specific improvements supported by research and evidence.

Additional Context

Field teams surveyed the Cascades loop in May, June, and July 2026. They documented trail erosion, damaged stream banks, informal creek access, and visitor behavior around wildlife. The draft recommends actions such as drainage repair, raised tread, hardened access points, revegetation, visitor messaging, and temporary closures.

The evidence is limited. The team did not conduct continuous visitor counts, winter or post-storm observations, water-quality testing, formal sensitive-species surveys, engineering analysis, or a complete accessibility assessment. The document should make clear which recommendations are supported now and which need more verification.
