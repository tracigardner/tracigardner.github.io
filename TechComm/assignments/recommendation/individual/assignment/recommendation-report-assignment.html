Recommendation Report
Assignment

Your Role in This Project

Worth 25% of your course grade

Write a recommendation report that explains how to improve the usability, document design, and accessibility of the client’s document, adapting the research plan from your proposal as needed. Use credible research and specific examples from the document to explain communication problems and recommend practical improvements.

You work as a consultant at Maroon + Orange Digital (M+D). You have been asked to develop recommendations for the client you discussed in your proposal assignment.

Review Markel & Selber Ch 18 for help with this project. Check your work with the Recommendation Report Self-Check as you work.

Assignment Learning Objectives

Hide the Learning Objectives Show the Learning Objectives

This assignment helps you:

  • Analyze a client document to identify communication problems and determine the needs of its organization and users.
  • Establish evaluation criteria using credible research, professional standards, and relevant guidelines.
  • Evaluate possible solutions by comparing evidence, identifying tradeoffs, and using a decision matrix.
  • Develop practical, persuasive recommendations supported by research and specific examples from the client document.
  • Organize a workplace recommendation report using the required sections and appropriate genre conventions.
  • Create effective visuals that help readers understand the findings and recommendations.
  • Apply plain language, document design, and accessibility principles to produce a clear, professional report that meets readers' needs.

Your Document

M+D’s Project Lead approved your proposal to recommend improvements to your client’s document and has asked you to write a recommendation report for the client.

The clients have staff with the expertise to address the content. They need recommendations that will improve how the documents communicate. Each client is particularly interested in document design and usability, plain language, and accessibility.

Focus on the same client document from the Scenario List that you chose for your proposal.

Your Audience

Write to the key contact at your client organization, who will decide whether to put your recommendations into action. Your key contact needs evidence of the problems, their consequences, and the practicality of the recommended solutions.

Keep in mind that two different audiences are important to this project:

  1. The key contact at the client organization is the audience for the recommendation report.
  2. The people who use the client document are an additional audience whose needs should guide your evaluation.

Assignment Requirements

Basic Requirements

  • Genre: Workplace-ready recommendation report.
  • Length: 9–15 pages, including all required content. Longer documents okay.
  • Layout: 1" margins all around. Include page numbers according to Page Numbers Infographic.
  • Document Design:
    • Single space within paragraphs.
    • Double space between paragraphs and around headings.
    • Do not indent paragraphs.
    • Use hierarchical headings to structure the body.
  • Plain Language: Use short sentences, chunked paragraphs, and other plain language techniques.

Content & Structure Requirements

  • Content: Follow sections and structure outlined in “TABLE 18.1 Elements of a Typical Report,” in Ch 18: Writing Recommendation Reports.
  • Required:
    • Letter of Transmittal
    • Title Page
    • Table of Contents
    • Abstract
    • Executive Summary
    • Introduction
    • Methods
    • Results
    • Conclusions
    • Recommendations
    • References
  • Optional: List of Illustrations, Glossary, List of Symbols, Appendices. Digital reports do not have covers.

Visuals Requirements

  • Number: At least three relevant visual aids, in addition to the Decision Matrix.
  • Examples: Photographs, screenshots, tables, charts, diagrams, and other relevant visuals. No clip art.
  • Location: On the same page and alongside or after the reference to it in the report body.
  • Caption: A two-part caption—a number and descriptive title—for each visual (for example, Figure 1: Current Navigation Structure, or Table 1: Accessibility Issues to Investigate).
  • Integration: Refer to each visual by number in the text before it appears (for example, “As Figure 1 shows…”).
  • Help: See Ch 12: Creating Graphics.

Research & References Requirements

  • Number: Use at least six outside sources.
  • Purpose & Use: Use your sources to establish criteria for evaluating the client document and to support your recommendations. Include relevant professional standards, accessibility guidelines, research findings, and other credible resources.
  • In-Text Citations: Cite information according to your discipline’s documentation style (e.g., engineers use IEEE, biologists use CSE).
  • References: Include a complete citation in your discipline’s documentation style. Do not list sources you have not cited within your report body.
  • Help: See Documentation and Citations for Your Projects as well as Ch 6: Researching Your Subject and App B: Documenting Your Sources.

Success Tips

  • Follow the explanations and examples in Ch 18: Writing Recommendation Reports of Markel & Selber.
  • Show, don’t just tell. Use annotated screenshots or brief before-and-after examples from the client’s document to demonstrate communication problems and illustrate possible improvements.
  • Use document design strategies that match what you recommend in your report (for instance, if you recommend informative headings for your client’s document, be sure you use them in your report).
  • Prioritize your recommendations according to their importance and feasibility.

Optional AI Feedback

  • Keep the original draft untouched.
  • Use a prompt that asks AI to review your draft and provide suggestions for improvement only. (No direct revisions)
  • Save the AI comments or revision advice. I may ask to see your AI feedback if I have a question.
  • Add a submission comment that identifies which AI tool(s) you used and how you used the tools (in other words, what did you ask AI to do?).
