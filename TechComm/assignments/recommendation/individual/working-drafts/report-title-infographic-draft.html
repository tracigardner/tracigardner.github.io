Build a Better Report Title

From Keywords to a Clear, Specific Title

A strong technical report title tells readers what the report is about and what to expect. It uses specific, informative words without extra clutter.

Which Title Helps the Reader?

Too Vague

My Recommendation Report

Names the assignment, but not the subject.
Specific and Informative

Recommendations for Revising the Young Alumni Committees Website

Identifies the work and the document being studied.

Build Your Title in Five Steps

Follow one example from a handful of research words to a finished recommendation report title.

1

Find Your Keywords

Start with words that describe your research subject.

What document or topic are you studying? Write down the words readers would use to recognize it.

Website Young Alumni Committees Revising
2

Identify Your Report Type

Add words that describe the work your report does.

Because this is a recommendation report, recommendations helps explain the report’s purpose.

Add a purpose word

Recommendations Revising Website
3

Make It Specific

Name the exact place, group, document, or situation.

General labels leave readers guessing. Replace them with the precise subject of your investigation.

A website
The Young Alumni Committees Website
4

Assemble Your Title

Choose the strongest words and put them in a readable order.

Combine the report’s purpose, the action, and the specific subject. Adjust word forms and add small connecting words as needed.

Recommendations for Revising the Young Alumni Committees Website

Working title

Recommendations for Revising the Young Alumni Committees Website

5

Trim the Excess

Remove words that repeat or add little meaning.

Try adding unnecessary phrases to the working title. Notice how much clearer the original is when those extras disappear.

Wordy version

Recommendations and Findings for Revising the Young Alumni Committees Website: A Guide to Updating the Language and Design

Finished title

Recommendations for Revising the Young Alumni Committees Website

Why it works: “Recommendations” already implies findings, and “revising” covers the proposed updates without listing them twice.

Watch Another Title Makeover

Sometimes a long opening phrase can become one direct word.

Before

How to Properly Redesign the Frith First-Year Makerspace Website

After

Redesigning the Frith First-Year Makerspace Website

What changed? “Redesigning” replaces “How to Properly Redesign.” The word “properly” adds no useful information.

The Final Title Test

Before submitting your report, read the title by itself. Ask:

Does it identify the subject?
Is it specific to this report?
Does it tell readers what to expect?
Can you cut any words without losing meaning?

Aim for specific + informative + clear + concise.
