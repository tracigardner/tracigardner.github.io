Decision Matrix • Quick Guide

Which Improvements Come First?

Use a weighted decision matrix to prioritize recommendations.

A decision matrix helps you compare several worthwhile improvements using the same criteria. The goal is to decide what to address first—not to discard every lower-ranked idea.

Example: A theater ticket email

Riverton Community Theater wants to improve its ticket confirmation email. Customers struggle to find essential details, understand arrival instructions, and use some links. Which changes should the theater prioritize?

Four steps to a decision

1

List improvements

Identify specific changes supported by your document investigation.

2

Choose criteria and weights

Decide what matters most. Give more important criteria higher weights.

3

Rate and calculate

Rate each improvement against every criterion. Multiply each rating by its weight, then add.

4

Set priorities

Use the totals and your evidence to explain which changes should come first.

1. Four possible improvements

APut the performance date, time, and location at the top.
BReplace vague links with descriptive link text.
CRewrite parking and arrival instructions in plain language.
DImprove the layout for reading on a phone.

2–3. Weigh, rate, and calculate

Use a simple 1–3 scale for both weights and ratings. A higher number always means more important or more favorable.

How a total works

For improvement A, multiply each rating by its criterion's weight:

(3 × 3) + (3 × 3) + (3 × 2) + (3 × 2) = 30

4. Interpret the priorities

In this illustration, the theater would start with A, followed by B, D, and C.

Prioritize—don't eliminate.

Lower scores do not mean an improvement is unnecessary. Explain tradeoffs, support your ratings with evidence, and address required accessibility corrections regardless of their rank.

For your recommendation report: Keep the matrix small (about four improvements and three or four criteria). Include it in your report and briefly explain how it informed your priorities. This theater example and its scores are fictional.
