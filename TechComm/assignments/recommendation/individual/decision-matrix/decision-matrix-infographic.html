Decision Matrix • Quick Guide

Which Improvements Come First?

Use a weighted decision matrix to prioritize recommendations.

A decision matrix compares worthwhile improvements using the same criteria to help decide what to address first.

A theater ticket email

Riverton Community Theater wants to improve its ticket confirmation email. Its fictional review identifies these problems and possible solutions:

What the theater found

What it could improve

Customers often miss the performance date and time.

A

Move key event details to the top.

An accessibility review found links labeled “Click here.”

B

Replace vague links with descriptive text.

Customers report difficulty reading the email on phones.

C

Improve the layout for mobile reading.

What matters most?

The theater chooses criteria based on customer needs and staff resources. It gives more important criteria greater weight.

  • Benefit to customers3Helping customers use their tickets is the main goal.
  • Urgency3Problems with essential information need prompt attention.
  • Ease of implementation2Staff time matters, but customer needs matter more.

Priorities determine the weights. Evidence informs the ratings.

Compare the improvements

Weight: 1 = less important; 3 = most important. Rating: 1 = less favorable; 3 = most favorable. Higher scores indicate higher priority.

One rating explained: Moving event details to the top earns a 3 for customer benefit because customers often miss that essential information. Its weight of 3 makes the weighted score 9 for this criterion. Add all weighted scores to get each total.

What comes first?

A · Event details (24)→ B · Link text (22)→ C · Mobile layout (14)

The theater would start with clearer event details, then improve link text and mobile layout. The scores guide its priorities; they do not replace professional judgment.

Prioritize—don't eliminate.

Lower-ranked improvements may still be necessary. Required accessibility corrections must be addressed regardless of their score.

In your report: Use a small matrix and briefly explain the criteria, weights, important ratings, and resulting priorities. All theater findings and scores above are fictional.
