Decision Matrix • Quick Guide

What Should We Fix First?

Compare improvements. Set priorities.

The situation

Riverton Community Theater’s ticket confirmation email is difficult to use. The theater has several possible improvements—but which should come first?

Possible improvements

AHighlight key details
Put the date, time, and location first.
BSimplify instructions
Make arrival directions easier to follow.
CClarify links
Replace vague links with descriptive text.

Compare using a decision matrix

Choose criteria, give each an importance weight, and rate each improvement. Use 1 (low), 2 (medium), or 3 (high).

Example ratings are fictional. Your ratings should reflect evidence from your investigation.

How scores work

Weight × rating for each row; then add the results. For A: (3 × 3) + (3 × 3) + (2 × 3) = 24

Use the scores to set priorities

AFirst24 points
CNext22 points
BThen18 points

Prioritize—don't eliminate.

Lower-ranked improvements may still be worthwhile. Required accessibility fixes should be addressed regardless of score.

In your report: Use a small matrix to support your priorities. Briefly explain your criteria and the evidence behind your ratings.
