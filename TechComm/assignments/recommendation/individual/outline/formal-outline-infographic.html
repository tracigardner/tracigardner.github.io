Build a Formal Outline

A formal outline is a plan for your document. It uses letters, numbers, and indentation to show which ideas are main points, which are subpoints, and how supporting details fit together. Build the outline before drafting so you can spot missing information and reorganize ideas easily.

Show the Document Hierarchy

Each step inward adds detail to the idea above it. Items at the same level have equal importance.

Follow the Outlining Rules

Divide Ideas into at Least Two Parts

If you have an A, you need a B. If you have a 1, you need a 2. Otherwise, keep the idea at the higher level.

✕ One subpoint
I. Results
   A. Navigation
✓ Two subpoints
I. Results
   A. Navigation
   B. Accessibility

Use Parallel Wording

At the same level, use the same grammatical form. Don't mix phrases beginning with nouns and verbs.

✕ Mixed forms
A. Navigation issues
B. Improving contrast
✓ Matching forms
A. Navigation issues
B. Contrast problems

Keep Details with Their Parent Ideas

Every indented point must explain, support, or divide the point directly above it. Move unrelated information to the right section.

Choose One Wording Style

A topic outline uses short phrases. A sentence outline uses complete sentences. Both are valid; use one style consistently unless your assignment specifies otherwise.

3. Build the Outline in Four Moves

01

Gather ideas

List problems, evidence, methods, and possible recommendations without worrying about order.

02

Group related ideas

Cluster findings about navigation, accessibility, plain language, or other relevant topics.

03

Add levels

Put major report sections at Level 1, categories at Level 2, and evidence or details beneath them.

04

Test the structure

Check logic, parallel wording, two-part divisions, and whether each point belongs where you placed it.

Quick Check Before You Draft

  • Do major points follow a logical order?
  • Does every subdivision have at least two items?
  • Are items at the same level parallel and equally important?
  • Does every supporting detail belong under its parent point?
  • Can a reader see the structure without reading full paragraphs?
