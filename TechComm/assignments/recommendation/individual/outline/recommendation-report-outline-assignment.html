Recommendation Report Outline
Assignment

Your Role in This Project

You work as a consultant at Maroon + Orange Digital (M+D). Your Project Lead has asked you to prepare a formal working outline for the recommendation report you are developing for your client.

Plan the complete report, including its headings and subheadings. Make your outline specific to your client document and the communication problems you are investigating. This is a planning document, not a draft of the full report; your ideas may change as your research continues.

Review Chapter 18 of Markel & Selber for the report structure. The instructions below explain the formal outline format, which the textbook does not cover.

Assignment Learning Objectives

This assignment helps you:

  • Plan the organization of a workplace recommendation report.
  • Develop informative, project-specific headings and subheadings.
  • Use formal outlining conventions to show the hierarchy of ideas.
  • Identify missing information and next steps before drafting.

Your Document

Create an outline for your individual recommendation report, using the same client document you selected for your proposal. M+D’s Project Lead has approved your investigation of how the client document communicates, particularly its document design and usability, plain language, and accessibility.

Your outline should plan the report you will actually write. Include topics, evaluation criteria, examples, and tentative findings that are relevant to your specific client document.

Refer to the Scenario List and your proposal as you plan.

Your Audience

Your outline is a working plan for yourself and a way for your instructor to see how your report is taking shape. It should make the planned organization and supporting details easy to follow.

Keep the audiences for the eventual recommendation report in mind: the key contact at the client organization, who will decide whether to act on your recommendations, and the people who use the client document, whose needs guide your evaluation. Choose report headings that will help the client contact find and understand the evidence and proposed improvements.

Assignment Requirements

Basic Requirements

  • Genre: Formal alphanumeric working outline for your individual recommendation report.
  • Length: As long as needed to show the complete planned structure and supporting details; no fixed page count.
  • Format: Use Roman numerals (I, II, III), capital letters (A, B), Arabic numerals (1, 2), and lowercase letters (a, b) for successive levels. Indent each level consistently.
  • Wording: Use concise, parallel phrases or brief notes. Complete sentences are not required.

Content & Structure Requirements

  • Report title: Start with a working title identifying the client document and the report’s purpose.
  • All required sections: Include Letter of Transmittal, Title Page, Abstract, Table of Contents, Executive Summary, Introduction, Methods, Results, Conclusions, Recommendations, and References, in the order required by the Recommendation Report Assignment.
  • Optional sections: Include a List of Illustrations, Glossary, List of Symbols, or Appendices only if you expect to use them. Do not add a cover for a digital report.
  • Specific headings: Include every heading and subheading you currently plan to use. Do not stop at labels such as “Methods,” “Results,” or “Recommendations.” Under those labels, identify the actual topics and evidence you will address.
  • Supporting details: Note the evaluation criteria, research methods, specific communication problems, potential improvements, and evidence you plan to discuss. For front matter, brief notes about planned content are enough.
  • Working status: Identify questions or tentative findings where research is unfinished. Do not invent results to fill the outline.

Formal Outline Structure

Use the same numbering system throughout. If you divide an item into subpoints, include at least two at that level (A and B, not A alone). Keep the items at the same level grammatically parallel.

Example: Part of a formal outline 
III. Methods
    A. Document design evaluation
        1. Heading hierarchy and navigation
        2. Tables and visual consistency
    B. Accessibility evaluation
        1. Relevant accessibility guidelines
        2. Review of document features

IV. Results
    A. Navigation and heading problems
        1. Inconsistent heading levels in [section]
        2. Difficulty finding [specific information]
    B. Accessibility findings
        1. Missing descriptions for [visuals]
        2. Unclear link text in [section]

This example illustrates the hierarchy and level of specificity. Your outline should reflect your own document and research, not copy these sample findings.

Success Tips

  • Start with the report requirements. Use Chapter 18, especially Table 18.1, and the Recommendation Report Assignment to check the complete structure.
  • Make the headings informative. For example, replace “Problem 1” with a heading such as “Inconsistent headings make the guide difficult to scan.”
  • Plan from evidence to action. Show how your methods and evaluation criteria lead to results, conclusions, and practical recommendations.
  • Note possible visuals. Mark where screenshots, tables, comparisons, or your decision matrix may help readers.
  • Keep the outline flexible. Add notes or placeholders for research you still need, then update the outline while drafting.

Optional AI Feedback

You may ask a generative-AI tool (such as Copilot, HokieAI, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude) to review your own outline for organization, completeness, consistency, and clarity.

  • Keep your original outline unchanged while you review the feedback.
  • Ask for suggestions only, not a rewritten outline.
  • Check all suggestions against your report requirements and your actual research.
  • Save the AI feedback in case I ask to see it. Add a submission comment identifying the tool and how you used it.

Submission Details

Save your outline as a PDF to preserve numbering and indentation. Upload the PDF to this assignment in Canvas.

Avoid these common errors

  • Don’t email your assignment. I grade all work in Canvas.
  • Don’t submit a link to a document. Upload the PDF file.
  • Don’t submit only a list of generic report section names. Include specific headings, subheadings, and planning details.
