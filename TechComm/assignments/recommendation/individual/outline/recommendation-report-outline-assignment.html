Recommendation Report Outline
Assignment

Your Task

Create a formal outline that maps out your individual recommendation report, from the letter of transmittal through the references and any optional back matter. Include all planned headings and subheadings, using details specific to your client document and investigation.

Your outline is a working plan, not a draft of the report. It should show what you intend to discuss and how the ideas fit together. You can revise it as your research develops.

Assignment Learning Objectives

This assignment helps you:

  • Plan the organization of a workplace recommendation report.
  • Develop specific, informative headings and subheadings for your analysis.
  • Use formal outline conventions to show relationships among sections and supporting points.
  • Identify gaps in your research and drafting plan.

Assignment Requirements

Content

  • Scope: Outline the entire report, including front matter, body, and back matter. Include all required sections from the Recommendation Report Assignment: Letter of Transmittal, Title Page, Table of Contents, Abstract, Executive Summary, Introduction, Methods, Results, Conclusions, Recommendations, and References.
  • Optional sections: Include a List of Illustrations, Glossary, List of Symbols, or Appendices only if you expect to need them.
  • Specificity: Go beyond generic labels. Under each major section, identify the specific topics, evidence, findings, or recommendations you expect to address. Include your planned report headings and subheadings.
  • Working details: Add short phrases or notes about planned content, including likely evaluation criteria, examples from the client document, research, visuals, and decision-matrix comparisons. Mark uncertain items as tentative rather than inventing findings.
  • Report title: Begin with a working title that identifies the subject and purpose of your report. You can refine it later.

Formal Outline Format

Use a conventional alphanumeric outline: Roman numerals for major sections, capital letters for subsections, Arabic numerals for supporting points, and lowercase letters for further detail. Indent each level consistently.

Use parallel wording for items at the same level. When you subdivide a point, include at least two items at that level (for example, A and B, not A alone). Short phrases are fine; full sentences are not required.

Example: Part of a working outline 
III. Methods
    A. Document design evaluation
        1. Heading hierarchy and navigation
        2. Tables, graphics, and visual consistency
    B. Plain language evaluation
        1. Sentence and paragraph clarity
        2. Terminology and definitions
    C. Accessibility evaluation
        1. Relevant accessibility guidelines
        2. Review of document features

IV. Results
    A. Navigation and heading issues
        1. Inconsistent heading levels in [document section]
        2. Difficulty locating [specific information]
    B. Plain language findings
        1. Unexplained terminology in [section]
        2. Dense paragraphs in [section]

This example illustrates structure, not required findings or headings. Tailor your outline to your client document and actual research.

How to Complete Your Outline

  1. Review your approved proposal, client scenario, and the Recommendation Report Assignment.
  2. Consult Table 18.1, “Elements of a Typical Report,” in Chapter 18 to confirm the required sections and their purposes.
  3. List the major report sections in the order they will appear. Add relevant optional sections only as needed.
  4. Develop specific headings and subheadings for the body. Identify the methods you used or plan to use, the criteria you are applying, the issues you are investigating, and the recommendations you are considering.
  5. Add supporting points or brief notes to indicate what will go under each heading. For front matter, indicate the planned content rather than writing the complete letter, abstract, or executive summary.
  6. Check that the outline is complete, specific to your project, and formatted consistently. Revise it as your investigation continues.

Before You Submit

  • Does the outline cover every required report section and any optional sections you plan to use?
  • Are the headings and subheadings specific enough to show what your report will actually discuss?
  • Does the outline use Roman numerals, letters, numbers, and consistent indentation?
  • Have you included enough detail to guide drafting without pretending that unfinished research is complete?

Submission Details

Save your outline as a PDF so its indentation and numbering remain intact. Upload the PDF to this assignment in Canvas. Do not submit a link or email the file.
