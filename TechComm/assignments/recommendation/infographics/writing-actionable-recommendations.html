Writing Actionable Recommendations

Turn your findings into clear, specific, and feasible recommendations that explain what should change and why.

What makes a recommendation actionable?

It identifies a change the client can make, connects that change to a documented problem, and explains the expected benefit. A strong recommendation also considers the client's resources, priorities, and constraints.

Five Steps for Writing Actionable Recommendations

1

Start with your findings

  • Identify the most important communication problems your investigation revealed.
  • Use specific observations, research, and evaluation criteria as evidence.
  • Prioritize problems that affect readers' ability to use the document.
2

Consider the client and users

  • Identify who will make the change and who will benefit from it.
  • Consider the document's purpose, the users' tasks, and the client's priorities.
  • Keep the recommendation within the scope of improving communication, rather than changing technical content outside your expertise.
3

Specify the change

  • Use a concrete action verb: reorganize, label, shorten, add, replace, or revise.
  • Name the affected section, feature, or element and explain how to change it.
  • Give enough detail for the client to understand the next step without inventing unsupported requirements.
4

Explain why it matters

  • Connect the proposed change to the finding and its implications.
  • Explain how it could improve usability, accessibility, readability, or another relevant criterion.
  • Support the reasoning with research or professional guidelines where appropriate.
5

Check feasibility

  • Consider time, staff effort, tools, and other practical constraints.
  • Suggest priorities or a phased approach when a change would be substantial.
  • Acknowledge trade-offs or uncertainties rather than promising results you cannot establish.

From Finding to Actionable Recommendation

Trace the reasoning from evidence to a change the client can make.

1. Finding · What did you find?

Important procedures are buried in long sections with vague headings.

2. Conclusion · Why does it matter?

The document's organization makes procedures difficult for employees to locate quickly.

3. Recommendation · What should change?

Reorganize procedures by employee task and replace vague headings with descriptive, task-focused headings so readers can find the instructions they need.

Make the action more useful

Vague: “Improve the organization of the guide.”

Actionable: “Group procedures by the tasks employees perform, such as reporting an incident or requesting equipment, and give each group a descriptive heading. This organization will help employees locate the appropriate instructions without scanning unrelated material.”

Before You Finalize a Recommendation

Specific: Does it identify exactly what should change?

Evidence-based: Can you trace it to a finding or evaluation criterion?

Reader-centered: Does it explain the benefit for document users?

Feasible: Can the client realistically act on it?
