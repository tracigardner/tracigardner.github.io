The Recommendation Formula

From Evidence to Action

A strong recommendation tells your client what to change, why it matters, and—when helpful—how to put it into practice. Use these formulas as flexible building blocks, not rigid sentence templates.

1

Build It: Write the Recommendation

Start with a clear action and a reason.

Action VerbReorganize, add, replace, label…
+
What ChangesName the part of the document.
+
Why It MattersIdentify the reader benefit.
Put the pieces together

Reorganize the onboarding guide by employee task so new engineers can find procedures more quickly.

2

Support It: Connect Evidence to Action

Use the logic of your report to justify the recommendation.

FindingWhat did you discover?
→
ImplicationWhat does it mean for users?
→
RecommendationWhat should the client do?
FindingFood-safety instructions appear in long paragraphs.
ImplicationShoppers may overlook important handling steps.
RecommendationConvert the handling instructions into a numbered checklist so shoppers can follow the steps in order.
3

Strengthen It: Add Implementation Details

Choose only the details your client needs to act.

Core RecommendationAction + change + reason
+
Useful DetailsHow, where, when, or priority
Possible additions:
How?Method
Where?Document location
When?Sequence or priority

Example: Add descriptive headings to the feasibility study’s financial assumptions section, grouping information by cost category. Prioritize this change before sharing the report with investors so they can locate key assumptions.

Check Your Formula

Can the client identify the action? Is it grounded in findings? Does the reason explain a reader benefit? Are the implementation details realistic? Not every recommendation needs a number, deadline, or all the optional pieces.
