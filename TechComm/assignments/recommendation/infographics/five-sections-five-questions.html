Five Sections, Five Questions

The five sections in the body of a recommendation report answer different questions. Together, they move readers from understanding the problem to deciding what action to take.

1

Introduction

What Is This Report About?

Establish the problem, purpose, background, and scope of the investigation.

Running example · Problem

Employees struggle to find important procedures.

2

Methods

What Did You Do?

Explain how you investigated the problem, gathered evidence, and established evaluation criteria.

Running example · Process

Evaluate navigation, headings, and organization against usability guidelines.

3

Results

What Did You Find?

Present the evidence from your investigation without interpreting its significance.

Running example · Evidence

Important procedures are buried in long sections with vague headings.

4

Conclusions

What Do the Findings Mean?

Interpret the evidence and explain its implications for the client and document users.

Running example · Meaning

The organization makes information difficult to locate quickly.

5

Recommendations

What Should We Do?

Propose specific, feasible improvements supported by the findings and conclusions.

Running example · Action

Reorganize procedures by task and add descriptive headings.

How the Sections Work Together

Each section builds on the previous one to create a logical argument for action.

Problem
Process
Evidence
Meaning
Action

Remember these guidelines:

  • Results report what you found.
  • Conclusions explain what those findings mean.
  • Recommendations explain what the client should do next.
