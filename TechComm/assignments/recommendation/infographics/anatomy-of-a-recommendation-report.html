Anatomy of a Recommendation Report

Three parts. Three different jobs. One complete report.

A recommendation report explains your investigation and recommends practical improvements. Its sections fall into three groups, each serving a different reader need.

Front Matter

Orient the reader. Introduce the report, summarize its key points, and help readers find information.

Elements in report order

  1. Letter of Transmittal
  2. Title Page
  3. Table of Contents
  4. Abstract
  5. Executive Summary
  6. List of Illustrations (optional)

Body

Make the case. Explain what you investigated, what you found, what it means, and what should happen next.

Elements in report order

  1. Introduction
  2. Methods
  3. Results
  4. Conclusions
  5. Recommendations

The body connects your evidence and evaluation to feasible recommendations.

Back Matter

Support the report. Document sources and supply additional information for readers who need more detail.

Elements in report order

  1. Glossary (optional)
  2. List of Symbols (optional)
  3. References
  4. Appendices (optional)
Glossary
List of Symbols

Appendix A

Include optional elements only when they help your readers.

Do not put “Front Matter,” “Body,” or “Back Matter” in your report.

These are names for groups of sections, not headings to insert into the finished document. Use the actual section titles instead.

✕ Not in your report
  • Front Matter
  • Body
  • Back Matter
✓ In your report
  • Executive Summary
  • Introduction
  • Methods
  • Results
  • References

How the parts work together

Body

Back Matter
