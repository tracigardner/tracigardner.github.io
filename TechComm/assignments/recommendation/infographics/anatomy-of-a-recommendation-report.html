Anatomy of a Recommendation Report

A recommendation report explains your investigation and recommends practical improvements. Its sections fall into three groups, each serving a different reader need.

Front Matter

Orient the reader. Introduce the report, summarize its key points, and help readers find information.

Elements in report order

  1. Letter of Transmittal
  2. Title Page
  3. Abstract
  4. Table of Contents
  5. Executive Summary
  6. List of Illustrations (optional)

Body

Make the case. Explain what you investigated, what you found, what it means, and what should happen next.

Elements in report order

  1. Introduction
  2. Methods
  3. Results
  4. Conclusions
  5. Recommendations

Back Matter

Support the report. Document sources and supply additional information for readers who need more detail.

Elements in report order

  1. Glossary (optional)
  2. List of Symbols (optional)
  3. References
  4. Appendices (optional)

Do Not Put Generic Labels in Your Report.

These are names for groups of sections, not headings to insert into the finished document. Use the actual section titles instead, like the examples below.

✕ Not in your report
  • Front Matter
  • Body
  • Back Matter
✓ In your report
  • Executive Summary
  • Introduction
  • References

How the Parts Work Together

Front Matter

  • What is this report about?
  • What are its key points?
  • Where can I find information?

Body

  • What did you do?
  • What did you find?
  • What does it mean?
  • What do you recommend?

Back Matter

  • Where did the evidence come from?
  • Where can I find supporting details?
