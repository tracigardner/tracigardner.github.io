Purpose & Audience
in Your Front Matter

Three parts of a recommendation report discuss the same project—but each serves a different reader and purpose. The three sections may overlap, but don't write the same summary three times.

Three Documents, Three Jobs

Letter of Transmittal

What report are you sending me, and why?

Audience
Client or primary recipient, typically the client contact or person who commissioned it.
Purpose
Introduce and deliver the report while establishing a courteous, professional relationship with its recipient.

Include: Why you’re sending it, what it covers, and a courteous closing. May briefly mention a key finding or recommendation.

Abstract

Is this technical report relevant to me?

Audience
Readers familiar with the technical subject, such as researchers and specialists.
Purpose
Summarize the investigation so readers can judge its relevance and decide whether to read the full report. Use an informative abstract for your recommendation report.

Include: Purpose, approach, major findings, and recommendations. Usually 200 words or fewer. May include searchable keywords.

Executive Summary

What does this mean for our organization, and what should we do?

Audience
Managers and decision-makers who need to understand the project’s organizational significance.
Purpose
Help managers decide what the findings mean and what action to take.

Include: Problem or opportunity, key findings, organizational consequences, and recommended next steps. Methods are usually only a sentence or two.
