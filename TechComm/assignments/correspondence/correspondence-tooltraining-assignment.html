Correspondence & Informational Report

Your Task

Worth 15% of your course grade

Write a letter to a client that provides a brief, objective informational report on a tool or training video they have asked about. Present neutral facts without advocating for any specific option or methodology.

Review Markel & Selber Ch 14 and 17 for help with this project. Check your work with the Correspondence & Informational Report Self-Check as you work.

Assignment Learning Objectives

By completing this assignment, you will learn how to plan and draft a professional, neutral-news letter that communicates your research intentions clearly and persuasively. You will practice organizing your message using a reader-focused structure (good news up front, details in the body, polite close). You will also develop your ability to:

  • Identify and articulate a clear and feasible research topic relevant to your field.
  • Justify the value and scope of your topic, including key questions you intend to explore.
  • Analyze a real-world non-expert audience and tailor your message to meet their needs in terms of content, tone, and design.
  • Practice appropriate professional letter etiquette, tone, and formatting to build credibility with your reader.
Technical documents must be as objective and unbiased as you can make them. If readers suspect that you are slanting information — by overstating or omitting facts, for example — they will doubt the validity of the entire document.
  • Select appropriate formats based on purpose and audience.
  • Adapt tone and style to different professional situations.
  • Structure information effectively for maximum impact.
  • Apply principles of document design and plain language.
  • Maintain professionalism across all communication channels.

    Your Audience

    Write your message to Traci (tengrrl@vt.edu), your teacher for the course. She’s the person who decides whether your topic is appropriate for the projects.

    Provide her with the basic details on the subject and clear, concrete details on how it fits the Ut Prosim project. This is a neutral-news message, but you still have to persuade her to approve your idea!

    Assignment Requirements

    In this message, aim for 4–5 concise paragraphs (roughly 250–350 words total, 12 pt font).

    • Project Format: Write a fake letter message in Word or Google Docs, using the formatting details in the Subject Selection Message Example to set up your fake message, which should look like a real letter.
    • Organization: Your message must include the following sections:
      • Salutation: Start with a polite greeting.
      • Introduction (1 short paragraph): Direct statement of purpose (the topic you plan to pursue). No heading.
      • Scope & Feasibility (1–2 paragraphs): Use “Scope & Feasibility” (14 pts bold, no quotation marks) as your heading. Explain why you have chosen this subject and why you believe it is important enough to research in depth. Sketch the key questions you expect to answer. (Don’t worry; they can change over time as you research!)
      • Audience Analysis (1-2 paragraphs): Use “Audience Analysis” (14 pts bold, no quotation marks) as your heading. Identify a real non-expert group (e.g., first-year majors, community members, patients, town council members, investors, etc.) and briefly explain the following information:

        • what they already know about your subject.
        • what they need to know about it.
        • why they care about it.
        • how you will need to approach them in terms of language and design.

        Remember the audience for your Subject Selection Message (Traci) is not the same as your Ut Prosim project audience (a real non-expert group).

      • Polite Close (1 paragraph): Ask for approval or guidance, thank the reader, and provide your contact info within a single paragraph. No heading.
      • Closing & Signature Block: Add a professional signature block with basic, relevant details.
    • Plain Language: Use short sentences and other plain language techniques.
    • Document Design: Chunk paragraphs; single-space within paragraphs; add one blank line between paragraphs (double space); do not indent paragraphs.

    Success Tips

    • Complete the Semester-Long Topic Test before you write your message. You can reuse text from this homework in your message.
    • Use a clear and objective tone. No need to be a cheerleader.
    • Use the required headings (Scope & Feasibility, and Audience Analysis).
    • Remember there are no headings for the Introduction or Closing.
    • Identify a real non-expert audience with specific needs for your Ut Prosim project.
    • Provide specific details on your topic. Details make your idea clear to your audience.
